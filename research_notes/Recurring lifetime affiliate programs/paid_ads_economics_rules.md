# Paid Ads (Google / Meta) for Recurring-Commission SaaS Affiliate Programs: Economics and Rules (as of Oct 2026)

Research note on method: support.google.com, manychat.com, gohighlevel.com, help.kajabi.com, wordstream.com and churnbuster.io were blocked by the research network proxy, so primary policy and terms pages could not be opened in full. Facts below come from search-engine extracts of those pages (marked "per search extract of official page") or from secondary sources (marked). A reader should open the official pages before acting. Roughly 18 tool calls were used.

## 1. Google Ads (and Meta) policies on affiliate ads: what is allowed, what gets accounts suspended

### Takeaway
Affiliate marketing is allowed on Google Ads, but the landing page must offer real original value. A thin "bridge page" that only forwards users to the merchant can be disapproved under "Destination requirements / Insufficient original content". Deceptive tactics such as implying you are the brand, cloaking or redirects that hide the real destination can get the whole account suspended, often without warning and permanently. Trademarks are not restricted as keywords under Google's own policy, but they are restricted in ad text unless you qualify as a reseller or informational site. Meta enforces similar rules under "Circumventing Systems" and "Unacceptable Business Practices".

### Cited Findings
- Google's "Insufficient original content" rule (part of Destination requirements) covers destinations that mainly show ads, copy content without adding value, exist mainly to send users elsewhere, or are incoherent. It explicitly includes "destinations solely designed to send users elsewhere" — [Travelpayouts summary](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/can-you-use-google-ads-for-affiliate-marketing/); [Search Engine Journal on Destination Requirements update](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ads-updates-destination-requirements-policy/439612/)
- A pure pass-through affiliate page with no original content can also be flagged under Google's "Abusing the ad network" policy. Pages that immediately send people to another site are labeled insufficient original content — [secondary: Ivan Mana guide](https://ivanmana.com/google-ads-affiliate-marketing/); [LinksTest affiliate suspension guide](https://affiliate-blog.linkstest.com/affiliate-ad-suspension-guide-google-meta-account-bans-beyond-disclosure/)
- Practices that help compliance include hands-on reviews, side-by-side comparisons, calculators or selection tools, and clear pricing context — [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/can-you-use-google-ads-for-affiliate-marketing/)
- Google's "Unacceptable business practices" policy (current page ID 15938071) covers misleading claims, false scarcity, and implying an affiliation with a brand you do not have. Violations are treated as "egregious": accounts are suspended on detection without prior warning and the advertiser is not allowed to advertise with Google Ads again — [Google Ads policy page (per search extract)](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938071); [Adcore](https://www.adcore.com/blog/suspended-google-ads-for-unacceptable-business-practices/)
- Google trademark policy: the restriction applies to trademarks in **ad text**. Trademarked **keywords** are not restricted by Google's policy, so anyone can bid on brand terms as far as Google is concerned. Resellers and informational sites may use a trademark in ad text if the landing page is primarily about that product (a reseller page must make the sale easy and show price; an informational page must mainly inform about the product). This exception applies to ads targeting the US, CA, UK, IE, AU and NZ, among others — [BrandVerity](https://www.brandverity.com/blog/google-adwords-trademark-policies-top-8-questions); [PPC Hero](https://ppchero.com/?p=20389)
- Meta "Circumventing Systems" policy: ads must not use tactics that disguise the ad's content or landing page, such as cloaking, redirects that hide the real destination, or extra accounts to evade restrictions. This is one of Meta's harshest enforcement categories and can lead to restrictions at the Business Manager level, flagged payment methods and blocked domains — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/pt-br/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/circumventing-systems); [Stubgroup (secondary)](https://stubgroup.com/blog/meta-circumventing-systems-suspension-what-it-means-and-how-to-address-it/)
- Meta "Unacceptable Business Practices": ads must not promote offers that use deceptive or misleading practices — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/fraud-scams/unacceptable-business-practices/)

### Inferences
- The low-risk model for Google is a real review or comparison page on your own domain (for example "best CRM for agencies" comparing several tools) with non-brand keywords and no brand names in ad text unless the page qualifies as informational. Direct-linking an ad to a merchant's affiliate URL, or using a thin "click here to see the offer" page, is the pattern most likely to be disapproved. Any wording that suggests you *are* the vendor risks a suspension with no warning.
- Even when Google's policy allows something (such as bidding on brand keywords), the affiliate program's own terms usually ban it (see section 2). The stricter rule wins.

### Gaps
- Could not open the official Google pages (Destination requirements, Trademarks, Unacceptable business practices) directly, so exact current wording and any changes made in 2025-2026 are unverified. Google has also required advertiser verification for several years; I did not confirm details for 2026.
- Found no official Google document that uses the exact term "bridge page". The term is industry shorthand that maps to "insufficient original content" and "abusing the ad network".

## 2. Common affiliate program rules: brand bidding, direct linking, trademark in domain, consequences

### Takeaway
The major programs ban bidding on their brand names in paid search unless you have written permission, and HighLevel also bans brand names in ad copy and display URLs. A breach usually leads to forfeited commissions and removal from the program. Programs on PartnerStack can reverse commissions before payout. The usual pattern is that refunds and chargebacks are deducted from future commissions.

### Cited Findings
- **HighLevel:** affiliates may not bid on HighLevel, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, or coupon/discount/promo/deal variations without written consent. They may not combine these terms with other words, or use them in ad title, copy, display name or display URL. HighLevel recommends adding them as negative keywords. Non-branded PPC is allowed — [NetPartners summary of HighLevel brand bidding policy](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-affiliate-brand-bidding-ppc-policy/) (secondary; the official page at gohighlevel.com was blocked)
- **HighLevel commission:** 40% recurring for the life of the customer on direct referrals, plus 5% tier 2. Payouts are monthly through Tipalti with a $50 minimum. Reported cookie length conflicts between sources (30 vs 90 days) — [UpPromote directory](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/highlevel/); [OneExpand](https://oneexpand.com/gohighlevel-affiliate-program/) (secondary)
- **ManyChat:** paid search ads using ManyChat trademarks or their variations as bidding terms are prohibited without prior written authorization. Paid ads are otherwise listed as an accepted traffic source. Commission is 30/40/50% recurring **for the first 12 months only** (tiers by lifetime paid signups: 0-30, 31-200, 201+). The cookie lasts 120 days. Payouts go through PartnerStack (Stripe or PayPal) — [Reditus](https://getreditus.com/affiliate-programs/manychat); [ManyChat affiliate terms (per search extract)](https://manychat.com/legal/affiliate-program-terms); [ManyChat Partner Program FAQ](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/20518248843164-Manychat-Partner-Program-FAQ)
- **PartnerStack clawbacks:** program managers can remove incorrect commissions from an invoice during the review window (1st-7th of the month). After an invoice is paid, the commission belongs to the partner, and PartnerStack itself will not pull money back out of a partner's withdrawal balance — [PartnerStack help: Clawbacks and incorrect commissions](https://support.partnerstack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047980474)
- **Typical refund clause (example: Sendcloud on PartnerStack):** a 30-day commission hold. Customer refunds or chargebacks are deducted from the affiliate's balance, and if the commission was already paid the company may deduct it from future payouts — [Sendcloud affiliate terms](https://www.sendcloud.com/en-uk/affiliate-program-terms/)

### Inferences
- In practice, the "net" commission is lower than the headline figure because refunds and chargebacks are subtracted and there is a hold of about 30 days before money is paid.
- For brand-bidding breaches, the usual remedy in affiliate agreements is to withhold commissions and terminate the affiliate. Read the specific program's terms.

### Gaps
- Could not verify an explicit **trademark-in-domain ban** (for example "highlevelreviews.com") or a **direct-linking ban** in the official HighLevel, ManyChat or Kajabi terms because those pages were blocked. These clauses are common across the industry but are unconfirmed here for these programs.
- Could not confirm Impact.com's standard clawback terms.
- Found no documented 2023-2026 case of a named affiliate losing commissions over brand bidding with one of these specific programs.

## 3. SMB SaaS churn and lifetime commission math; data for HighLevel / Kajabi / ManyChat

### Takeaway
Low-priced SMB SaaS typically loses about 3-7% of customers per month. One commonly cited benchmark is a median of about 6.1% monthly churn for ARPA under $25 versus about 2.2% for ARPA above $500. Because of churn, a "lifetime" commission usually pays for about 14-33 months on average, not forever. Programs capped at 12 months (such as ManyChat) pay even less. None of the three named platforms publishes a reliable churn figure.

### Cited Findings
- Median monthly customer churn is about 6.1% for businesses with ARPA under $25/month and about 2.2% for ARPA above $500/month. ChartMogul suggests the best companies aim for under 2% a month — [Churn Buster 2026 benchmarks (secondary, citing ChartMogul-type data)](https://churnbuster.io/articles/b2b-saas-churn-rate/); [Subjolt](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/churn-rate-benchmarks/)
- SMB SaaS churn is said to average about 4.5% monthly, or about 42% a year — [RetentionCheck (secondary aggregator)](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/smb-saas)
- In Recurly's network, about a third of SaaS churn comes from failed payments (involuntary churn) — [Churn Buster citing Recurly](https://churnbuster.io/articles/b2b-saas-churn-rate/)
- Kajabi, HighLevel and ManyChat are private and do not publicly disclose churn. One template-marketing site states "Kajabi... cut churn to 6.2% in FY2025", but there is no primary source and it should be treated as unreliable — [canvasbusinessmodel.com (unreliable)](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/products/kajabi-marketing-mix); [Tella](https://www.tella.com/definition/kajabi-churn-rate)

### Inferences (my own arithmetic, not a sourced figure)
- Expected number of paying months is roughly 1 divided by the monthly churn rate: 3% churn gives about 33 months, 5% about 20 months, and 7% about 14 months.
- Hypothetical example: a $97/month subscription with a 40% lifetime commission pays $38.80/month. Expected total commission is about $1,290 at 3% churn, $776 at 5% and $554 at 7%. This is before refunds, before plan changes, and without discounting money received later.
- With a 12-month cap (as ManyChat has), the expected total is the sum over months 1-12 of commission × (1 − churn)^(month − 1). At 5% churn that is about 9.2 months of commission, not 12.
- Practical break-even check: maximum ad cost per *paying* customer ≈ expected total commission. For example, at a $5 CPC and $776 expected commission, you need at least 1 paying customer per ~155 clicks (a click-to-paid rate of about 0.65%) just to break even.

### Gaps
- Could not open the ChartMogul, Recurly or Baremetrics primary reports, so the 6.1% and 2.2% figures are attributed through secondary sources.
- No verifiable churn data was found for HighLevel, Kajabi or ManyChat customers.

## 4. CPC and trial-to-paid conversion benchmarks for SaaS keywords

### Takeaway
WordStream/LocaliQ's 2025 Google Ads benchmarks put the all-industry average CPC at about $5.26, up from $4.66 in 2024; Business Services is about $5.58 and Education is about $6.23. SaaS and B2B software keywords are usually more expensive, about $6-$14 for non-brand solution terms according to agency sources. Trial-to-paid conversion is about 18-25% for trials without a credit card and about 49-60% when a card is required.

### Cited Findings
- WordStream/LocaliQ 2025: average CPC across all industries $5.26 (2024: $4.66). Business Services $5.58, Education & Instruction $6.23, Career & Employment $5.16, Finance & Insurance $3.46. The dataset covers 16,000+ campaigns — [WordStream 2025 Google Ads benchmarks (per search extract)](https://www.wordstream.com/resources/google-ads-industry-benchmarks)
- A conflicting figure: one report summarizes CPC as rising to $5.42 in 2025, with conversion rates up to 8.18% — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ads-cpcs-rose-to-5-42-in-2025-with-87-of-industries-seeing-increases-but-conversion-rates-climbed-to-8-18-as-automation-improves/). These numbers may come from a different WordStream release or a different cut of the data. Not reconciled.
- B2B SaaS: branded keywords $1-$3, competitor brand terms $5-$25, mid-intent solution keywords $6-$12, and high-intent buying terms often above $15 — [Powered by Search (agency)](https://www.poweredbysearch.com/learn/b2b-saas-google-ads-stats-benchmarks/)
- The median non-brand SaaS CPC in 2026 is about $8.50-$14; project-management and devtools terms run about $7-$9 — [GrowthSpree (agency blog, 2026)](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/saas-google-ads-benchmarks-2026-cpc-cpl-ctr-conversion-rate-by-vertical)
- Trial-to-paid: opt-out trials (card required) convert at about 48.8% and opt-in trials (no card) at about 18.2% (Q1 2022-Q3 2025 data). Another 2026 study reports 60.4% and 25.2%. Opt-out signup volume is about 30% of opt-in volume — [Visionary Marketing 2026](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026); [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=1723)

### Inferences
- An affiliate funnel loses people at more steps than a vendor's own funnel: ad click, then your page, then a click-through to the merchant, then trial signup, then a paid conversion that is attributed to you. If 30% click through to the merchant, 10% of those start a trial and 25% of trials convert, the click-to-paid rate is about 0.75%. At a $6-$10 CPC that means about $800-$1,300 per paying customer, which is roughly break-even or a loss against the commission estimates in section 3. These step rates are illustrative assumptions, not sourced benchmarks.

### Gaps
- Found no Semrush or Keyword Planner CPC figures from a credible source for the exact keywords "CRM for agencies" or "course platform". Live checks in Keyword Planner are needed.
- Found no published click-to-paid conversion data for SaaS *affiliate* funnels.

## 5. Risks: term changes, ended lifetime commissions, shutdowns, clawbacks (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Programs change their terms often. Kajabi moved new partners off its 30% lifetime rate to tiered 10-20% lifetime (after 100% of the first month), and keeps the old rate only for grandfathered partners who stay active. Canva closed its affiliate program in January 2024. Several programs (Kit/ConvertKit, HubSpot, GetResponse, Webflow) reportedly pay only for the first 12 months. "Recurring" rarely means forever.

### Cited Findings
- Kajabi's current structure is 100% of the first month, then 10-20% recurring for the customer's lifetime depending on partner tier. The legacy 30% lifetime rate survives only for grandfathered partners, and they lose it if they hit the inactivity rule. Kajabi also removed a cash bonus for 50-100 active referrals — [SchoolMaker review 2026 (secondary)](https://www.schoolmaker.com/blog/kajabi-affiliate-program); [Reditus](https://getreditus.com/affiliate-programs/kajabi); [Kajabi Partner Program FAQ (official, blocked; per search extract)](https://help.kajabi.com/en/articles/12696571-kajabi-partner-program-faq)
- Canva closed its affiliate program as of January 2024. Semrush retired its old 40% recurring BeRush model. Kit, HubSpot, GetResponse and Webflow pay for 12 months. Kit moved to 50% for the first 12 months followed by a tiered 10-20% lifetime rate — [secondary aggregators surfaced via search, including commissiondex.com and affiliate-times.com](https://affiliate-times.com/convertkits-rebrand-to-kit-what-affiliates-need-to-know-in-2026/); [Commissiondex Kit](https://commissiondex.com/programs/convertkit-affiliate/). These are aggregator claims and should be checked against official announcements.
- Notion previously discontinued its affiliate program and later relaunched it at 50% for 12 months on net-new paid upgrades — [NotionApps (secondary)](https://www.notionapps.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-notion-affiliate-program-2025)
- Refunds and chargebacks are commonly deducted from current or future commissions — [Sendcloud terms](https://www.sendcloud.com/en-uk/affiliate-program-terms/); see section 2

### Inferences
- When judging a paid-ads plan, treat "lifetime" commissions as revocable. A model that recovers ad spend only after 12 or more months is exposed to term changes. Payback within a few months is far safer.

### Gaps
- Exact effective dates of the Kajabi and Kit changes were not confirmed from primary announcements.
- No documented HighLevel or ManyChat term changes from 2023-2026 were found in this pass.

## 7. Payout taxes and forms (brief)

### Takeaway
US-based affiliates usually submit a W-9; non-US affiliates usually submit a W-8BEN (individuals) or W-8BEN-E (entities) through the payout platform (Tipalti for HighLevel, PartnerStack for ManyChat). The platform collects these forms before paying out.

### Cited Findings
- HighLevel pays through Tipalti and ManyChat through PartnerStack — [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/highlevel/); [Reditus](https://getreditus.com/affiliate-programs/manychat)

### Inferences
- The W-9 versus W-8BEN split is standard US IRS practice for payees. It was not re-verified from IRS pages in this session.

### Gaps
- No session source was fetched for IRS form rules, 1099 thresholds or treaty withholding. Check with irs.gov or a tax advisor.
