# HighLevel Affiliate Program: Paid Ads and Direct Linking (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for gohighlevel.com, help.gohighlevel.com, support.google.com, netpartners.marketing and botpenguin.com. **No page could be read in full.** Every official fact below comes from search-engine snippets of the official page, which are paraphrased or summarized by the search tool. Treat even "[official snippet]" items as close paraphrase, not verified verbatim text. Labels: [read in full] (none achieved), [official snippet], [third-party].

Short answer: **No, you cannot direct-link from a paid ad.** HighLevel's affiliate terms ban sending PPC ad clicks straight to HighLevel's website, including through redirects. Ad traffic has to land on "an actual page on the affiliate's Website." You also cannot bid on HighLevel brand terms, use them in ad text or display URLs, outbid HighLevel in positions 1-5 on any search term, or run paid search on competitors' trademarks. Breaking these rules can get you removed from the program right away and cost you all unpaid commissions. Google's own bridge-page policy points the same way: the landing page must offer real value of its own.

## 1. HighLevel's official affiliate terms on PPC, keywords, ad text and direct linking

### Takeaway
HighLevel's affiliate agreement bans bidding on a defined list of "Restricted Terms" and their variations or misspellings on any ad network unless HighLevel gives written consent. The same terms are banned in ad titles, ad copy, display names and display URLs. Direct-linking or redirecting PPC traffic to HighLevel is also banned. The program rules add two more limits: no PPC bid that appears above HighLevel in positions 1-5 for any term, and no paid-search technique built on competitors' trademarks.

### Cited Findings
- [official snippet] "Unless HighLevel gives you written consent to do otherwise, you may not bid on any of HighLevel's Restricted Terms, including any variations or misspellings thereof for search or content-based campaigns on Google, Bing, MSN, Yahoo, Facebook or any other network." — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms) (also surfaced via [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules))
- [official snippet] Definition of Restricted Terms: "HighLevel, HighLevel Inc, gohighlevel.com, www.gohighlevel.com, HighLevel coupon, HighLevel discount code, HighLevel discount, HighLevel promo code, HighLevel sale, HighLevel promo, HighLevel sales, HighLevel deals, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, and any other terms that HighLevel may deem a Restricted Term in the future." — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms). Note: one third-party summary lists "HighLevel LLC" in place of "HighLevel Inc." and leaves out GoHighLevel, GHL and SaaSPRENEUR, which suggests an older version ([HighLevel affiliate-terms-old page in search results](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms-old); summary via search tool). The current list as returned for /affiliate-terms includes GoHighLevel, GHL and SaaSPRENEUR.
- [official snippet] Affiliates may not use Restricted Terms, "including any variations or misspellings in sequence with any other keyword," or use them "in ad title, ad copy, display name or as the display URL." — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)
- [official snippet] **Direct linking:** affiliates may not direct "links to HighLevel's Website from any PPC ad or [use] redirects that yield the same result; customers must be directed to an actual page on the affiliate's Website." — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)
- [official snippet] Affiliates may not "bid in any manner appearing higher than HighLevel for any search term in positions 1-5 in any auction-style PPC advertising program." — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [official snippet] Affiliates may not engage in "unlawful or deceptive actions with respect to search engine optimization, including using any technique that generates paid search results based on any trademarks of HighLevel, any brand name of HighLevel, or based on the trademarks or brand name of any competitor of HighLevel." — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [official snippet] Affiliates may not offer "discounts, coupons, promo codes, free trials, prizes, or other incentives related to HighLevel unless expressly authorized in writing," and may not run contests, sweepstakes or giveaways that reference HighLevel without prior written approval. — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [official snippet] FTC disclosure: the relationship must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously." On Instagram and Facebook, the disclosure goes at the start of the caption, before the "read more" cut-off. — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [official snippet] Banned tracking fraud: cookie stuffing, forced redirects, pop-ups meant to set cookies, masking referral URLs, and claiming credit for sales the affiliate did not influence. — [HighLevel affiliate terms/policies, search snippet](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [official snippet] **Consequences:** if HighLevel finds an affiliate out of compliance, it may terminate the agreement and the affiliate's participation immediately, "including forfeiture of earned commissions." If terminated for cause, the affiliate forfeits all right to unpaid commissions. — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)
- [third-party] HighLevel "recommends adding its trademarked terms as negative keywords" to avoid accidental matches. — [NetPartners: GoHighLevel Affiliate Brand Bidding and PPC Policy](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-affiliate-brand-bidding-ppc-policy/) (search summary only; page blocked)
- [third-party] The agreement was described as "last updated in April 2023." — search-tool summary of [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms). Not verified.
- [third-party, contradicts official] One guide recommends sending traffic "directly to the GHL trial page" through the affiliate link to keep tracking reliable. — [autogencrm.com guide](https://autogencrm.com/gohighlevel-affiliate-marketing/) (search summary). This is fine for organic traffic but **would breach the PPC direct-linking clause** if used for paid ads.

### Inferences
- **Direct linking from Google, Bing, Meta or YouTube PPC ads is banned.** The final URL must be a real page on your own site, such as a landing page, review, tutorial, or lead-magnet opt-in. The HighLevel affiliate link then goes on that page.
- **Brand terms:** the network list explicitly names Facebook and "any other network," so the keyword ban plus the ban on brand names in ad text covers Meta and YouTube ads too. A plain reading says you cannot write "HighLevel" or "GoHighLevel" in the ad copy itself, whatever Google's trademark policy allows.
- **Negative keywords:** the official snippets don't show a mandatory negative-keyword clause. Only a third-party source says it is "recommended." It is still the practical way to comply with the ban on terms in sequence with other keywords and misspellings, given broad and phrase match.
- **Competitor "alternative" keywords** (for example "ClickFunnels alternative," "Kajabi vs," "HubSpot alternative") look **banned** under the competitor-trademark clause. Generic, unbranded terms ("CRM for agencies," "white label CRM," "marketing automation for agencies") are not on the restricted list. They are still subject to the positions 1-5 clause. Read literally, that clause means you cannot outrank HighLevel's own ads on any term HighLevel also bids on, which is hard to monitor.
- The "free trials" incentive clause is ambiguous. Promoting HighLevel's own trial delivered through the affiliate link is probably fine. Inventing your own trial or bonus offer that names HighLevel needs written approval. Confirm with HighLevel.
- With written consent, any of these rules could be waived. The terms keep reserving "written consent."

## 2. Google Ads policies affecting affiliates (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Google disapproves ads whose destinations are bridge, doorway or gateway pages, or whose content isn't original. An affiliate landing page needs real value of its own, which matches HighLevel's no-direct-link rule. Google's trademark policy allows resellers and informational sites to use a third party's trademark in ad text in some cases, but HighLevel's contract bans it regardless.

### Cited Findings
- [official snippet] **Insufficient original content:** ads are disapproved when destination content is "designed for the primary purpose of showing ads," is "replicated from another source without adding value," or when destinations are "solely designed to send users to another site (including bridge pages, doorways, gateways, or other intermediate pages)." Examples include mirroring, framing, scraping, and templates or pre-generated sites with duplicate content. — [Google: Insufficient original content](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427718?hl=en)
- [official snippet] Promoting "bridge" or "gateway" destinations designed solely to send users elsewhere is a form of **Abusing the ad network**. — [Google: Abusing the ad network](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020954?hl=en)
- [official snippet] Ad destinations "must offer unique value to users and be functional, useful, and easy to navigate." — [Google: Destination requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6368661?hl=en)
- [official snippet] **Destination mismatch:** an ad is disapproved when the domain in the display URL doesn't match the final URL's domain. — [Google: Destination mismatch](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16428020?hl=en)
- [official snippet] **Trademarks:** resellers or informational sites may use others' trademarks in some cases, but "the ad and landing page must be clear as to whether the advertiser is a reseller or informational site." Google won't restrict the trademark when the landing page is primarily dedicated to selling or clearly facilitating the sale of the trademarked product (with commercial information such as prices), or when its primary purpose is "to provide informative details about products or services corresponding to the trademark." Google will restrict use by direct competitors and use that is "confusing, deceptive, or misleading." Owners can authorize or complain through the Trademark Troubleshooter. — [Google: Trademarks](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118?hl=en); [Trademark Troubleshooter](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/troubleshooter/14327740?hl=en)
- [official snippet] **Business name:** "Third-party resellers, independent booking intermediaries, affiliate distributors and secondary sellers ... are prohibited from using the standalone brand name of the goods, services or properties that they offer as their business name." — [Google: Business name requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14847993?hl=en) (snippet surfaced from Google adspolicy search results)
- [official snippet] Google's Terms require a separate account for each end-advertiser an agency manages, and say this "may also restrict ad delivery within auctions." — [Google Ads Terms & Conditions](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/54818?hl=en)
- [official snippet, existence only] Policy pages for Unacceptable business practices ([15938071](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938071?hl=en)) and Misrepresentation ([6020955](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?hl=en)) exist, and an "Update to editorial policy (October 2026)" page is listed ([18287059](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/18287059?hl=en-IE)). Their text couldn't be retrieved.

### Inferences
- Even without HighLevel's rule, a Google ad pointing straight at an affiliate redirect (display URL = your domain, final URL = gohighlevel.com) risks a destination mismatch disapproval. A thin "click here for the trial" page risks an insufficient original content or bridge-page disapproval. A compliant setup is a substantive page: a review, comparison, tutorial, case study, or lead magnet with real content.
- Your Google business name or advertiser name must not be "HighLevel" or "GoHighLevel" (business-name policy, plus HighLevel's display-name ban).
- Impersonation-style ads (looking like HighLevel itself) would fall under Google's Misrepresentation and Unacceptable business practices policies (impersonating brands). The exact wording couldn't be fetched.

- Couldn't retrieve current wording on advertiser verification (2023+) for affiliates.

## 3. Legitimate paid-ad strategies, trial offers and CPC ranges

### Takeaway
A compliant approach is: generic non-brand keywords (no HighLevel or competitor trademarks), sent to your own content or lead-magnet page with FTC disclosure, which then links on to the HighLevel affiliate link. Third-party sites widely report that affiliate links give a 30-day trial instead of the standard 14 days. HighLevel hasn't confirmed this here. I found no credible CPC data for these specific keywords.

### Cited Findings
- [third-party] The standard trial is 14 days from gohighlevel.com. An "extended 30-day free trial" is distributed through affiliate partner links, applies automatically with no promo code, and requires a credit card. — [automatethejourney.com](https://automatethejourney.com/blog/gohighlevel-free-trial-2026); [netpartners.marketing](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-30-day-free-trial/); [oneexpand.com](https://oneexpand.com/gohighlevel-30-day-trial/); [thestackinsiders.com](https://www.thestackinsiders.com/blog/gohighlevel-free-trial) (search summaries only)
- [official snippet] HighLevel bans affiliates from offering "free trials ... or other incentives related to HighLevel unless expressly authorized in writing." — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)
- [third-party] Generic benchmark: B2B/SaaS average search CPC is about $2.30 (range $0.90-$4.80, up 9% year over year). The all-industry average rose from $4.66 to $5.42 in 2025. — [metricnexus.ai Google Ads Benchmarks 2026](https://metricnexus.ai/blog/google-ads-benchmarks-2026); [shopifreaks summary](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ads-cpcs-rose-to-5-42-in-2025-with-87-of-industries-seeing-increases-but-conversion-rates-climbed-to-8-18-as-automation-improves/) (search summaries only, methodology unverified)

### Inferences
- Allowed keyword examples (by plain reading): "CRM for marketing agencies," "white label CRM," "agency SaaS platform," "all-in-one marketing software," "SMS marketing for agencies," "funnel builder."
- Not allowed: anything containing HighLevel, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, gohighlevel.com, or misspellings, plus coupon, promo, discount or deal variants. Competitor brand terms (ClickFunnels, Kajabi, HubSpot, Vendasta, and so on) also look banned under the competitor-trademark clause.
- Ad copy should describe the category or benefit (for example "White-label CRM for agencies: free 30-day trial guide") without naming HighLevel. Whether "30-day free trial" in ad copy counts as an affiliate-offered "incentive" needing written authorization is unclear, so ask HighLevel's affiliate team in writing.
- Common compliant funnels (inferred, no official source): a lead magnet (snapshot, template or checklist) leading to an email sequence and then the affiliate link, a webinar or VSL, and a review or tutorial page. Building the page on your own domain satisfies both HighLevel's "actual page on the affiliate's Website" rule and Google's original-content rule.
- Generic CRM keywords such as "CRM for agencies" are probably well above the B2B/SaaS average, but this is unverified.

## 4. Meta and YouTube ads in brief

### Takeaway
HighLevel's network list explicitly includes Facebook and "any other network," so the brand-term and direct-linking rules apply to Meta and YouTube ads. Meta separately requires landing pages that match the ad and bans deceptive redirects.

### Cited Findings
- [official snippet] HighLevel's Restricted Terms ban covers "Google, Bing, MSN, Yahoo, Facebook or any other network." — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)
- [official snippet] Meta: products or services in the ad must match the landing page, and the landing page must work and be relevant. Deceptive redirects (for example, automatically redirecting to a substantially different domain) and misleading links are banned. — [Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/); [Meta Unacceptable Business Practices](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/fraud-scams/unacceptable-business-practices/); [Meta ad policy guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/m/small-business/ad-policy-guidance)
- [official snippet] HighLevel requires FTC disclosure at the start of Facebook and Instagram captions. — [Affiliate Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)

### Inferences
- YouTube ads run through Google Ads, so Google's destination and trademark policies apply, along with HighLevel's no-direct-link and no-brand-term rules. An organic YouTube video can name HighLevel. A paid YouTube ad most likely cannot use the brand name in ad text or titles.
- Bing/Microsoft Ads is explicitly named in HighLevel's terms, so the same rules apply.

### Gaps
- No Microsoft Ads or YouTube-specific affiliate policy text was retrieved.
- Whether HighLevel treats creatives showing the HighLevel UI (not the name) as brand use is unknown.
