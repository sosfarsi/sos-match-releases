**These notes are not legal advice.** The proxy blocked direct fetches of gohighlevel.com, support.google.com and netpartners.marketing, so no primary page was **[read in full]**. Each fact carries one of these labels:
- **[official snippet]**: a search-engine excerpt of an official page (ftc.gov, gohighlevel.com, linkedin.com, help.firstpromoter.com).
- **[third-party]**: a law firm, vendor or blog source.

Treat every HighLevel affiliate-term claim as something to check in your own affiliate dashboard (affiliate.gohighlevel.com) before you rely on it.

## Q1. Laws: what is allowed and what is forbidden (FTC, CAN-SPAM, TCPA/FCC, A2P, GDPR/UK/CASL, cookies)

### Takeaway
- **Email:** cold B2B email is legal in the US under CAN-SPAM (an opt-out regime) if you follow its rules, and is narrower in the UK, EU and Canada.
- **Texts and calls:** cold texting or autodialed calling to cell phones without prior express consent remains high-risk or forbidden under the TCPA. The FCC's 1:1 consent rule was vacated in Jan 2025, but the underlying consent requirement still applies.
- **Disclosure and reviews:** every affiliate link needs a clear and conspicuous disclosure. Fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials can now draw civil penalties.

### Cited Findings
**FTC Endorsement Guides (2023 update)**
- Affiliate links are a "material connection" that requires clear and conspicuous disclosure. "Clear and conspicuous" means difficult to miss and easily understandable. In interactive electronic media the disclosure must be "unavoidable". [third-party] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/ftc-state-ag/ftc-updates-endorsement-guidance-for-online-disclosures-and-influencer-programs/); [InfoLawGroup, Jul 2023](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/7/31/ftc-releases-updated-endorsement-guides-10-key-takeaways)
- Per the FTC FAQs as summarized:
  - "affiliate link" alone, or a "buy now" button, is not adequate disclosure.
  - "paid link" next to the link can be adequate.
  - "commissionable link" is not clear.

  [third-party] — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/ftc-finalizes-updated-guidance-for-influencer-and-consumer-review-programs.html)
- For video, the FTC recommends disclosing inside the video and also in the description near the links. Platform built-in disclosure tools may not be sufficient. [third-party] — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/ftc-finalizes-updated-guidance-for-influencer-and-consumer-review-programs.html)

**FTC fake reviews and testimonials rule (16 CFR Part 465)**
- The FTC finalized the rule Aug 14, 2024, and it took effect Oct 21, 2024. It bans fake or misleading reviews and testimonials, including:
  - AI-generated ones.
  - Ones from people who don't exist or had no real experience with the product.
  - Ones that misrepresent the reviewer's experience.

  It also bans compensation conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment. [third-party] — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [WSGR](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials.html)
- Knowing violators face civil penalties of up to $51,744 per violation (the 2024 figure; it is inflation-adjusted yearly). [third-party] — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews)

**Earnings claims**
- On Jan 13, 2025, the FTC proposed a new Earnings Claim Rule. It also proposed expanding the Business Opportunity Rule to "money-making opportunities", such as business coaching. Commissioners Ferguson and Holyoak (now the majority leadership) voted no, so finalization was uncertain. [third-party] — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/ftc-proposes-changes-to-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-by-multilevel-marketers-and-money-making-opportunity-sellers/); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1570786/ftc-puts-pedal-to-the-metal-to-release-proposed-rule-on-mlm-earnings-claims-changes-to-business-opportunity-rulebut-republican-commissioners-pump-the-brakes-in-potential-foreshadowing)

**CAN-SPAM (email)**
- There is no B2B exemption. The core requirements are:
  - accurate header and "From" information
  - non-deceptive subject lines
  - identifying the message as an ad
  - a valid physical postal address
  - a working opt-out, honored within 10 days
  - responsibility for what third parties send on your behalf

  [official snippet] — [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- Penalties are up to $53,088 per email (2026 inflation-adjusted figure, as reported by a third party; the FTC page snippet said "up to $53,088"). [official snippet / third-party] — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business); [Adaptive Security](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/email-compliance-penalties)

**TCPA and FCC consent rules (texts and calls)**
- On Jan 24, 2025, the Eleventh Circuit (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*) vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule days before its Jan 27, 2025 effective date. The court held that "prior express consent" carries its common-law meaning: voluntary, clearly stated, and given before the call. [third-party] — [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule)
- FCC revocation rules:
  - **In effect since Apr 11, 2025:** consumers may revoke consent "in any reasonable manner". Keywords such as STOP, QUIT, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE and END conclusively revoke consent. [third-party] — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-issues-one-year-limited-waiver-of-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/tcpa-connect/fcc’s-revocation-rule-to-take-effect-april-2025)
  - **Delayed to Jan 31, 2027 (order of Jan 6, 2026):** the "revoke-all" provision, under which an opt-out from one message type applies to all of the sender's robocalls and robotexts. The FCC is still considering modifying it. [third-party] — [TCPAWorld](https://tcpaworld.com/2026/01/07/breaking-fcc-pushes-back-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule-new-effective-date-now-january-31-2027/); [Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/fcc-extends-compliance-deadline-for-revoke-all-rule/)
- The revocation rules apply to B2B outreach as well. [third-party] — [Manatt](https://manatt.com/Insights/Newsletters/TCPA-Connect/FCC’s-Revocation-Rule-to-Take-Effect-April-2025)

**Outside the US (email)**
- **Canada (CASL):** the usual B2B path is *implied consent by conspicuous publication*. All three conditions must hold:
  1. The business address is publicly published.
  2. There is no statement refusing commercial messages.
  3. The message is relevant to the recipient's role.

  [third-party] — [Mystrika CASL guide](https://blog.mystrika.com/casl-cold-email-canada-guide/); [Substance Law](https://substancelaw.ca/casl-compliance/)
- **UK (PECR + UK GDPR):** marketing to individuals (including sole traders) is opt-in, with a narrow "soft opt-in" for existing customers and similar products. Corporate subscribers can be emailed without prior consent but must get an opt-out. [third-party] — [Whali](https://whali.co.uk/blog/is-cold-emailing-legal)
- **EU (GDPR):** B2B cold email can rest on legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f), with a documented Legitimate Interest Assessment, role relevance, identification and an opt-out. National ePrivacy laws vary; for example, Germany is stricter, which is my inference and needs a lawyer. [third-party] — [Whali](https://whali.co.uk/blog/is-cold-emailing-legal)

### Inferences
**Allowed (US)**
- Cold B2B email to agency owners, if it is CAN-SPAM compliant (ad identified, physical address, working unsubscribe, honest subject line).
- Texting or calling people who opted in through your own form with clear consent language.
- Honoring any reasonable opt-out.

**Forbidden or high-risk**
- Autodialed or AI-voice cold calls and cold texts to cell numbers without prior express consent. TCPA statutory damages are commonly cited at $500–$1,500 per message (my knowledge, not verified this session).
- Buying "consented" lead lists.
- Fake or AI-generated testimonials.
- "Make $10k/mo with HighLevel"-style claims. These are deceptive under FTC Act Sec. 5 regardless of the proposed rule's status, and they are separately barred by HighLevel's terms (Q3).

**Disclosure wording**
- Put "I earn a commission if you sign up through my link (paid link)" above the fold or before the first link, and say it inside videos.
- "Affiliate link" alone is not enough.

**Automation**
- Opt-out handling must be automated and cover keywords on every channel. Building now to the Jan 31, 2027 revoke-all standard is cheap insurance.

### Gaps
- **A2P 10DLC:** I did not get to source current registration rules (brand plus campaign registration through TCR, carrier vetting, the US carrier ban on unregistered 10DLC traffic). My understanding is that unregistered A2P traffic on 10DLC numbers is blocked by US carriers. Unverified this session; confirm in HighLevel's LC Phone docs.
- **Cookie consent:** not researched. My general knowledge, unverified: an EU/UK audience requires consent before non-essential cookies, which includes affiliate tracking and ad pixels; the US generally does not, but several state privacy laws require an opt-out for "sale/sharing" of data for targeted ads. Needs a lawyer or CMP vendor.
- **Earnings rule status:** whether the FTC's proposed Earnings Claim Rule was finalized or withdrawn by Oct 2026 was not found.
- **State laws:** state "mini-TCPA" laws (Florida FTSA, Oklahoma, etc.) were not researched. They can be stricter than federal rules, so a lawyer is needed before any SMS or calling program.

## Q2. Platform rules: Google Ads and Search, Meta, YouTube, LinkedIn, Reddit, email deliverability

### Takeaway
- **Google Ads:** allows affiliate landing ("bridge") pages only if they add substantial original value. Thin redirect pages get disapproved or the account suspended.
- **Google Search:** penalizes mass low-value pages ("scaled content abuse", "thin affiliation") regardless of whether AI wrote them. Since May 2026 this explicitly covers AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- **LinkedIn:** forbids automation tools outright.
- **Reddit:** tolerates only light, contribution-first self-promotion.
- **Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft:** enforce SPF, DKIM and DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, and a complaint rate under 0.3%.

### Cited Findings
**Google Ads**
- Google's Unacceptable Business Practices / Misrepresentation policies target destinations that omit relevant product information or mislead. [official snippet] — [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938071)
- Bridge pages are allowed but scrutinized. Pages that only redirect get disapproval or suspension; they need meaningful original content, comparisons and navigation. [third-party] — [Digistore24](https://www.digistore24.com/blog/affiliate-marketing-google-ads/)

**Google Search spam policies**
- The policies include:
  - scaled content abuse
  - site reputation abuse
  - thin affiliation
  - doorway pages
  - scraping
  - cloaking
  - link spam

  [third-party] — [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/google-spam-policies-explained)
- Scaled content abuse means mass pages made mainly to manipulate rankings. It applies whether the text was written by AI, freelancers or templates. AI content is not inherently against the rules; manipulative use is. [third-party] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/); [InsideA](https://insidea.com/blog/seo/googles-spam-policies-for-ai-generated-content)
- Google updated its documentation May 15, 2026: the spam policies explicitly apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. [third-party] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-spam-policies-now-officially-cover-ai-overviews-and-ai-mode-in-search)
- An August 2026 Google spam update occurred, with case studies of hit sites. [third-party] — [GSQi](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/)

**Email deliverability (Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft)**
- Since Feb 2024, senders of more than 5,000 emails a day to Gmail or Yahoo must:
  - authenticate with SPF and DKIM
  - publish DMARC, with the From domain aligned
  - provide one-click unsubscribe via the List-Unsubscribe header
  - honor unsubscribes within 2 days
  - keep spam complaints under 0.3% (target under 0.1%)

  [third-party] — [Bird](https://bird.com/docs/knowledge-base/deliverability/gmail-yahoo-requirements); [dmarcian](https://dmarcian.com/yahoo-and-google-dmarc-required/)
- Enforcement has tightened:
  - Gmail ramped up rejections of non-compliant traffic starting Nov 2025.
  - Microsoft (Outlook, Hotmail, Live) has enforced similar bulk-sender rules since May 5, 2025, rejecting non-compliant mail.

  [third-party] — [CaptainDNS](https://www.captaindns.com/en/blog/gmail-bulk-sender-2025); [InboxAlly](https://docs.inboxally.com/compliance-industry-updates/)

**LinkedIn**
- User Agreement §8.2 prohibits:
  - scraping
  - using "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to … add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts"
  - browser plug-ins or extensions that automate activity

  Violations risk restriction or shutdown of the account. [official snippet] — [LinkedIn Help: Prohibited software](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387); [ConductAtlas summary of User Agreement](https://conductatlas.com/platform/linkedin/linkedin-user-agreement/prohibition-on-scraping-and-automated-data-collection/)

**Reddit**
- Content Policy Rule 2 prohibits spamming and content manipulation. The long-standing guidance is "It's perfectly fine to be a Redditor with a website, it's not okay to be a website with a Reddit account." The 9:1 or 10% self-promotion ratio is community and old-wiki guidance; individual subreddits set stricter rules. [third-party] — [Redship 2026 guide](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules-2026)

### Inferences
**Google Ads**
- Send traffic to your own content-rich landing page (comparison, tutorial, snapshot lead magnet), never straight to a bare redirect.
- Write accurate ad copy about trial length and price. HighLevel's official trial is 14 days; see Q5.

**SEO with AI**
- Allowed: AI-assisted drafting with original screenshots, real testing, unique data and an editor's review, at a modest pace.
- High-risk: hundreds of templated "HighLevel for [niche]" or "HighLevel vs X" pages with little unique value. That pattern matches scaled content abuse or thin affiliation.

**LinkedIn**
- Manual posting and manual DMs only, or LinkedIn's own scheduling and ad tools.
- Tools like auto-connect or auto-DM extensions breach the ToS. They are not illegal, but they put the account at risk.

**Reddit**
- No automated posting.
- Answer genuinely in r/gohighlevel-type or agency subs and follow each sub's rules on affiliate links. Many subs ban them.

**Email infrastructure**
- Use a separate sending domain or subdomain for cold email.
- Keep cold volume per mailbox low so complaints stay under 0.3%.

### Gaps
- **Meta ads:** Special Ad Categories and landing-page and "business opportunity / get-rich" restrictions were not researched this session. My unverified knowledge: Meta's Advertising Standards restrict "deceptive and misleading practices", including income claims. Special Ad Categories (credit, employment, housing, social issues, financial products) are likely not triggered by SaaS software ads.
- **YouTube:** the paid-promotion disclosure checkbox and spam/deceptive-practices policies (e.g., misleading links, comment spam) were not fetched.
- **Google Ads specifics:** whether Google Ads currently requires advertiser verification for affiliates, and its trademark policy on bidding on other brands' terms (Google generally does not block keyword bidding on trademarks; ad-text use can be restricted), are unverified.

## Q3. HighLevel affiliate rules relevant to automation

### Takeaway
HighLevel's affiliate agreement bars the following, with termination and forfeiture of earned commissions as the penalty:
- unsupported product claims
- calling HighLevel a "business opportunity" or "business-in-a-box"
- income guarantees
- unlawful advertising or email (CAN-SPAM)

Brand-name PPC bidding is widely reported as banned. I could not read the exact PPC, competitor-keyword or incentive clauses because the site was blocked; verify them in the dashboard.

### Cited Findings
- From the HighLevel Affiliate Agreement [official snippet] — [HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms):
  - Affiliates may not make product claims "inconsistent with, or beyond the scope of" HighLevel's own marketing materials.
  - Affiliates are expressly prohibited from claiming HighLevel is "a business opportunity, franchise opportunity, a 'business-in-a-box,' or an assisted marketing plan."
  - Affiliates may not make claims that expressly or impliedly guarantee a new user will make money by using HighLevel or by becoming an affiliate.
  - Advertising must strictly comply with all laws, "including … email marketing laws (including the federal CAN-SPAM Act)."
  - Non-compliance is a material breach: HighLevel may terminate immediately, "including forfeiture of Earned Commissions".
- Brand bidding, and brand use in ad copy or display URLs, should be treated as banned unless HighLevel states otherwise in writing. The source recommends checking the dashboard terms' paid-search and trademark sections. [third-party] — [NetPartners (search snippet)](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-affiliate-brand-bidding-ppc-policy/)
- Program mechanics [third-party] — [OpenAffiliate](https://openaffiliate.dev/programs/gohighlevel); [Affililist](https://www.affililist.com/affiliate/gohighlevel); [NetPartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-affiliate-program/):
  - Runs on FirstPromoter at affiliate.gohighlevel.com, with `?fp_ref=` links. [official snippet for the portal](https://affiliate.gohighlevel.com/home)
  - 40% recurring commission.
  - Cookie reported as 90 days (sources conflict).
  - About 5% second-tier commission on recruited affiliates.
  - $50 minimum payout.
  - No approval or audience minimum.

### Inferences
- **Organic competitor content vs PPC:** organic comparison content naming competitors ("HighLevel vs ClickFunnels") is not barred by any clause I found. The found clauses restrict *claims accuracy*, not naming competitors. The reported restrictions are on *paid search* brand bidding. Competitor content must still be truthful and consistent with HighLevel's own materials, with no unsupported superiority or income claims.
- **Competitor-keyword PPC:** whether HighLevel bans affiliates from bidding on competitor brand terms (e.g., "ClickFunnels") is UNVERIFIED. Treat it as prohibited until confirmed in writing by HighLevel affiliate support.
- **Business-opportunity framing is the biggest automation trap.** Webinars and funnels pitching "start a SaaS agency and make $X/month" collide with both the FTC and HighLevel's "business opportunity" and income-guarantee bans.

### Gaps
- Exact clause text on:
  - paid search (brand terms, misspellings, competitor terms, direct linking)
  - trademark domains
  - incentives, rebates and bonuses (does it need written approval?)
  - coupon sites
  - self-referral
  - required disclosure wording
  - spam (SMS and social)

  Couldn't be read because gohighlevel.com was blocked. Must verify in the affiliate dashboard or with HighLevel affiliate support before launch.
- Cookie length conflict: 90 days in most third-party sources, with "older sources" reporting otherwise.

## Q4. Compliant automation options (SEO, YouTube, email nurture, webinars, retargeting, non-brand Google Ads, conversion postbacks, AI content)

### Takeaway
The lowest-risk automated engine:
1. Your own content (SEO plus YouTube tutorials and comparisons) leads to an opt-in lead magnet, such as free snapshots or templates.
2. A consented email nurture, sent with SPF, DKIM and DMARC plus one-click unsubscribe, leads to an evergreen webinar or demo.
3. Retargeting and non-brand Google Ads point to your own value-adding landing page.

Passing actual HighLevel sign-ups back to Google Ads is technically possible via FirstPromoter postbacks with `fp_sid`/gclid. Whether HighLevel's program enables postbacks for affiliates is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- **How FirstPromoter postbacks work** [official snippet] — [FirstPromoter Help: postbacks](https://help.firstpromoter.com/en/articles/9625829-how-to-set-up-postbacks-on-firstpromoter); [FirstPromoter changelog](https://changelog.firstpromoter.com/postbacks-PkVva):
  - They fire HTTP requests on new referral (sign-up), new customer (first payment) or new commission.
  - They substitute dynamic variables such as click ID and amount.
  - The click ID must be passed as `fp_sid` on the affiliate link.
- For Google Ads, the postback only fires when there is a valid gclid. [third-party] — [wecantrack FirstPromoter integration](https://wecantrack.com/firstpromoter-integration/)
- Postbacks are reported to be available only on FirstPromoter Enterprise plans. That is the merchant's plan (HighLevel's), not the affiliate's. [third-party] — [wecantrack](https://wecantrack.com/firstpromoter-integration/)
- AI content is acceptable to Google if it isn't mass-produced primarily for rankings (see Q2). [third-party] — [InsideA](https://insidea.com/blog/seo/googles-spam-policies-for-ai-generated-content)

### Inferences
**Conversion tracking**
- Most reliable regardless of postback access: track your own on-page conversion (lead magnet opt-in or webinar registration) in Google Ads and Meta, and optimize to that.
- If HighLevel's FirstPromoter setup exposes postbacks to affiliates, append `&fp_sid={gclid}` to your link and point the postback at a conversion-import endpoint (e.g., a server-side tagging or Zapier step that calls the Google Ads offline conversion import). Otherwise, upload conversions manually or offline from dashboard exports, where gclid is retained.
- Uploading conversion data to ad platforms involves personal data. EU users need consent (see Q1 gaps on cookies).

**Email nurture**
- Use only an opt-in list, captured with clear consent copy that names what they'll receive and that it includes affiliate recommendations.
- Include a physical address and one-click unsubscribe.
- If adding SMS, use a separate unchecked SMS-consent checkbox with TCPA-style disclosure language. Have a lawyer review the wording.

**Evergreen webinars**
- Allowed if:
  - "evergreen" or recorded status isn't misrepresented as live (my inference from FTC deception principles, not sourced)
  - there are no income guarantees or "business opportunity" framing
  - testimonials are real, from users with genuine experience, and typical results are not misrepresented

**AI at scale**
- Use AI for outlines, drafts, transcripts-to-posts and repurposing YouTube into blogs.
- Add original screenshots, step-by-step tests and personal opinions.
- Publish at a human-review pace.
- Never generate fake reviews, personas or testimonials (FTC rule, Q1).

### Gaps
- Whether HighLevel's affiliate portal exposes postback or `fp_sid` features to affiliates is UNKNOWN. HighLevel's FirstPromoter plan tier and settings aren't public. Ask HighLevel affiliate support.
- Retargeting-pixel rules (Meta/Google custom audiences, sensitive-category restrictions) were not researched in detail.

## Q5. What top HighLevel affiliates publicly do

### Takeaway
Prominent affiliates run:
- dedicated bonus or "offer" pages hosted on gohighlevel.com subpaths or their own domains
- extended 30-day trial links (vs the 14-day public trial)
- large stacked bonus bundles (snapshots, bootcamps, coaching)
- YouTube tutorial and comparison content

Bonus stacking appears tolerated in practice, but the written-approval requirement for incentives could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- HighLevel's public site offers a 14-day trial, and affiliate links commonly offer 30 days. One third-party source contradicts this, saying there is no official 30-day trial. [third-party] — [ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/software/gohighlevel/free-trial/); [GHL Experts](https://ghlexperts.com/gohighlevel-free-trial)
- Pavel Ketsuk (an affiliate page hosted on the gohighlevel.com domain) advertises a "30-day FREE trial + $12,786 in bonuses", including prebuilt websites, funnels and automations. [official snippet (affiliate page on HighLevel domain)] — [gohighlevel.com/pavel-ketsuk](https://gohighlevel.com/pavel-ketsuk)
- GHL Experts advertises "$11,270+ in free bonuses", including a "Quickstart Bootcamp" (stated value $997) and a "SaaSPreneur Quickstart Snapshot" (stated value $1,497), plus coaching and done-for-you snapshots. [third-party] — [GHL Experts bonuses](https://www.ghlexperts.com/bonuses)
- Common YouTube tactics include tutorials and "why I switched" comparison videos with the link in the description. [third-party] — [GHL Experts](https://ghlexperts.com/gohighlevel-free-trial)

### Inferences
- Snapshot and template bonuses are the de facto competitive norm and double as a lead magnet. Get written confirmation from HighLevel that your bonus offer is permitted under the incentive clause before promoting it.
- Inflated "valued at $X" bonus claims carry FTC deception risk if the values aren't substantiated. Use real, defensible values or omit them.
- The "SaaSPreneur" framing sits close to HighLevel's ban on "business opportunity" claims. Keep it about software capabilities, not guaranteed income.

### Gaps
- Specific top YouTube channels' subscriber counts and current tactics were not researched (time and tool limits).
- Whether HighLevel's terms explicitly require prior written approval for bonuses or incentives is unverified (site blocked). The research brief suggests they do; confirm in the dashboard.
