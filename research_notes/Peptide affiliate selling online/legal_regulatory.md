# Legal and Regulatory Risks of Selling or Promoting Peptides Online (US, as of October 6, 2026)

## 1. FDA status of "research use only" (RUO) peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, etc.) and the 2023–2026 Category 2 saga

### Takeaway
Selling BPC-157, TB-500, GLP-1 analogs and similar peptides with "research use only / not for human consumption" labels is **not a legal safe harbor**. They are unapproved new drugs, and FDA judges intended use from the marketing as a whole: dosing info, injection guides, dose calculators, benefit claims. The 2026 changes (Category 2 removals in April, the PCAC vote in July) only affect whether **licensed compounding pharmacies** may make them on a prescription. They don't legalize retail or "research" sales for human use, and as of early September 2026 no rule adding them to the 503A list had been proposed.

### Cited Findings
**Timeline (dated):**
- **Sept 2023:** FDA put BPC-157, TB-500 and 17 other peptides in "Category 2" of its 503A bulk-substance categories (substances that raise significant safety risks, so compounding is discouraged or effectively blocked). — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-advisory-committee-set-weigh-taking-action-certain-unapproved-peptides); [FDA PCAC briefing doc](https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download)
- **Late Feb 2026:** HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said publicly (including on the Joe Rogan Experience) that about 14 of the 19 restricted peptides would move back toward Category 1, i.e. eligible for compounding. Secondary sources date this to Feb 27, 2026. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/peptide-regulation-debate-intensifies-after-fda-advisory-panel-s-controversial-vote); [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-advisory-committee-set-weigh-taking-action-certain-unapproved-peptides); date detail from a clinic blog, [AgeMD](https://www.agemd.com/insights/longevity/rfk-bpc-157-fda-peptide-reclassification-2026) (lower reliability)
- **April 15, 2026:** In an update to its 503A categories, FDA removed **12 peptides** from Category 2, including BPC-157 and ipamorelin. Each removal took effect after seven calendar days because "the nominations were withdrawn by the nominators." These peptides were set for review at future advisory committee meetings. — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-advisory-committee-set-weigh-taking-action-certain-unapproved-peptides); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-considers-adding-a-dozen-peptides-to-its-bulk-drug-compounding-list.html). Secondary sources give a Federal Register notice of about April 16 and an effective date of about April 23, 2026 (not verified against the Federal Register). — [Lifetime Surgical blog](https://www.lifetimesurgical.com/blog-posts/fda-peptide-regulations-2026-changes)
- **April 29, 2026:** STAT reported critics' concerns that Kennedy was "stacking the deck" on FDA peptide oversight ahead of the policy shift. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/04/29/rfk-jr-peptides-upcoming-fda-policy-shift-compounding-drugs/)
- **July 23–24, 2026:** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) reviewed seven peptides. It voted to recommend six for the 503A Bulks List: **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon and Semax**. The BPC-157 vote was reported as 8–6 with one abstention. FDA staff scientists had recommended against adding any of the seven, and the committee went against that advice. — [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisory-committee-votes-add-popular-peptide-bpc/story?id=134913891); [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/07/23/fda-committee-peptides/); [NCPA](https://ncpa.org/newsroom/qam/2026/07/31/fda-advisory-committee-nominates-six-peptides-pharmacies-compound); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-panel-backs-6-peptides-for-compounding); [Buchanan Ingersoll (bipc.com)](https://www.bipc.com/fda-pcac-recommends-six-peptides-for-the-503a-bulks-list-what-compounding-pharmacies-need-to-know)
  - **Conflict:** PharmExec's headline says the panel voted to loosen restrictions on **four** peptides — [PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-votes-loosen-restrictions-four-peptides). This may reflect a single day's votes or a different count. Most sources say six.
- **Status after the vote:** PCAC votes are advisory and change nothing legally. FDA would have to publish a proposed rule, take comments and issue a final rule. As of early September 2026, **no proposed rule had been published**. — [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/fda-voted-yes-on-six-peptides-now-what-the-regulatory-road-ahead); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/news/fda-pcac-peptides-503a-compounding/). One secondary source estimates the rulemaking will take 8–12 months — [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisory-committee-votes-add-popular-peptide-bpc/story?id=134913891)

**RUO labeling and intended use:**
- In the Aug 2026 warning letters, firms had labeled products "for research, laboratory, and analytical use" and "not for veterinary or human use." FDA still found intended human use from the dosing information, injection instructions and dose calculators on their websites. — [McDermott Will & Schulte](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/); [ArentFox Schiff, "RUO-ined"](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy)
- Even after the April 2026 Category 2 removals, BPC-157, TB-500 and the others are still unapproved new drugs and cannot legally be sold for human use. — [promarketer.ca](https://www.promarketer.ca/post/google-ads-for-peptides) (marketing agency, lower reliability; consistent with law-firm analyses above)
- BPC-157 is on the WADA prohibited list, and USADA and the DoD's OPSS describe it as an unapproved drug that has been found in "wellness" products. — [USADA](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/bpc-157-peptide-prohibited/); [OPSS](https://www.opss.org/article/bpc-157-prohibited-peptide-and-unapproved-drug-found-health-and-wellness-products)

### Inferences
- "Legal to compound" (possibly, after rulemaking, by a licensed 503A pharmacy on a valid prescription for an individual patient) is a completely different channel from an online RUO storefront. **None of the 2026 changes legalize the RUO vendor model.**
- An affiliate site that publishes dosing guides, "protocols" or benefit claims creates the same intended-use evidence FDA cited against vendors.
- Watch items that may change after this date: an FDA proposed rule on the six peptides, decisions on the other peptides removed from Category 2, and any HHS policy statements.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch FDA's Category 2 page or the Federal Register notice directly, so the exact list of the 12 removed peptides and the precise publication and effective dates are unverified.
- I found no reliable report of FDA action between the July vote and October 6, 2026. **[Verify before relying on it.]**

## 2. Compounded GLP-1s (semaglutide, tirzepatide) and "research" GLP-1 sellers

### Takeaway
Both GLP-1 shortages ended (tirzepatide Dec 19, 2024; semaglutide Feb 21, 2025), and FDA's grace periods for compounding "essentially copies" ran out in spring 2025. In 2026 FDA sent waves of warning letters to telehealth marketers (30 in March, 25 in June) and to RUO GLP-1 sellers (7 in April, 5 more posted Sept 1). It also proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B list. Retatrutide, which is not approved anywhere, is a particular enforcement focus.

### Cited Findings
- **Dec 19, 2024:** FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved. 503A pharmacies had until Feb 18, 2025 and 503B outsourcing facilities until Mar 19, 2025 to stop. On Mar 5, 2025 a federal court denied the compounders' preliminary-injunction motion. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize); [American Med Spa Assn](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/compounded-glp-1-faq/)
- **Feb 21, 2025:** FDA declared the semaglutide shortage resolved. Enforcement discretion ended Apr 22, 2025 (503A) and May 22, 2025 (503B). — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize); [McDermott](https://www.mwe.com/?p=311415)
- **Sept 2025:** FDA sent warning letters to GLP-1 telehealth marketers, e.g. [CureX, 09/09/2025](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/curex-09092025).
- **Mar 3, 2026:** FDA issued 30 warning letters to telehealth firms over misleading promotion of compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, including "sameness" claims comparing them to approved drugs and claims about where the drugs come from. Firms got 15 days to respond, and FDA warned of seizure or injunction. — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters); [PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/why-the-fda-issued-30-warning-letters-to-telehealth-sites-marketing-compounded-glp-1s); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fda-takes-aim-at-misleading-online-claims-for-compounded-glp-1-drugs/)
- **Mar 31, 2026:** FDA warning letter to Gram Peptides. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)
- **April 2026:** FDA published seven warning letters to peptide sellers marketing GLP-1 analogs such as retatrutide and tirzepatide. — [ChemVerify](https://www.chemverify.com/learn/fda-research-use-only-enforcement-warning-letters-2026) (vendor-adjacent; count not verified on fda.gov)
- **Apr 30 / May 1, 2026:** FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B Bulks List (Federal Register, May 1, 2026). — [Federal Register 2026-08552](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/01/2026-08552/list-of-bulk-drug-substances-for-which-there-is-a-clinical-need-under-section-503b-of-the-federal); [glp1clinics.org](https://www.glp1clinics.org/blog/fda-30-warning-letters-glp1-compounding-crackdown-2026-03-29). Final status unknown.
- **June 8, 2026:** FDA warning letter to Altru Telehealth. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/altru-telehealth-llc-dba-altru-telehealth-728274-06082026). **June 16, 2026:** about 25 more telehealth letters covering semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide claims. — [Pharmaceutical Commerce](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/fda-issues-30-warning-letters-to-telehealth-firms-over-misleading-compounded-glp-1-marketing) (via search summary; verify the count)
- **Aug 24, 2026 letters (posted Sept 1, 2026):** FDA warned five online sellers: Peak Performance Peptides, Royal Peptides LLC, NuScience Peptides LLC, Peptide Partners LLC, and TXP Innovations LLC (d/b/a Tex Peptides), all based in AZ, FL, NC and TX. Products named included semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, survodutide, mazdutide, SS-31, PT-141, tesamorelin, ipamorelin blends, and bacteriostatic water sold for reconstitution. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/); [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy); [SpaKinect](https://www.spakinect.com/news/fda-warning-letters-peptide-glp1-products)
- FDA has stated that retatrutide and cagrilintide are not FDA-approved for any condition and cannot be used in compounding. — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260814/eli-lilly-files-lawsuits-against-six-companies-for-allegedly-selling-retatrutide) and related coverage

### Inferences
- RUO GLP-1 sellers, especially of retatrutide, face the most concentrated combined enforcement: FDA letters plus civil suits by drug makers (see section 3). Promoting retatrutide or "research semaglutide" is probably the riskiest niche an affiliate could pick.
- Even legitimate telehealth GLP-1 affiliates are exposed, because FDA's 2026 letters targeted marketing claims ("same as Ozempic," "FDA-approved ingredient"), which is exactly the kind of copy affiliates write.

## 3. Enforcement record: FDA, DOJ, private suits, states, influencers

### Takeaway
Enforcement escalated through 2025–2026. It now includes FDA warning letters, criminal FDCA misbranding prosecutions (including a prison sentence and a physician indictment), drug-maker lawsuits, and state board and attorney-general actions. I found **no public case charging a pure affiliate or influencer** for peptide promotion. Exposure flows mainly to vendors, clinics and physicians, but the legal theories (see section 5) can reach promoters.

### Cited Findings
- **DOJ, W.D. Michigan (July 23, 2026 sentencing):** Brandon Piper of Gobles, MI, was sentenced to 21 months for conspiracy to introduce misbranded drugs with intent to defraud or mislead. The scheme imported unapproved prescription drugs, including peptides, from China. — [DOJ USAO-WDMI](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmi/pr/2026_0723_Piper_Sentencing_PR)
- **DOJ, D. Utah (Apr 1, 2026 indictment):** A Utah osteopathic physician (Dr. Watkins) was indicted for receiving misbranded, non-approved peptides from China and selling them to more than 200 patients. Charges include receipt and delivery of misbranded drugs with intent to defraud, and aiding and abetting. Analysts called it a shift to criminal FDCA enforcement. — [DOJ USAO-UT](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china); [Stevens & Lee](https://www.stevenslee.com/health-law-observer-blog/indictment-of-osteopathic-physician-signals-shift-in-peptide-enforcement/)
- **DOJ, D. Maryland:** The owner of bodybuilding-drug companies was indicted for selling misbranded drugs. This is the older template for "research chemical" cases. — [DOJ USAO-MD](https://www.justice.gov/usao-md/pr/owner-bodybuilding-drug-companies-indicted-selling-misbranded-drugs)
- One law-firm summary reports a case that ended in a **70-month sentence plus $5 million forfeiture** where RUO disclaimers failed. The case isn't named in the snippet. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/)
- A "BioPeptide Labs" 2025 wire-fraud and misbranding indictment is reported only by [PeptideLaws.com](https://peptidelaws.com/news/recent-doj-actions-against-illegal-peptide-distributors) (unverified; I couldn't confirm it on justice.gov).
- **Eli Lilly civil suits (Aug 12, 2026):** Six lawsuits against Aesthetic Envy Cosmetic Centers, Astra, Legendary Peptides, Striker Pharmacy, Texas Peptides and Lone Star Peptide over retatrutide sold as "research-use only" but intended for humans.   - Ohio Board of Pharmacy guidance (Dec 2025): RUO-labeled products are unlawful for clinics and med spas to possess, even with patient consent. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/)
  - Alabama Medical Board notice (June 2026) on sourcing. ## 4. FTC rules: endorsements, affiliate disclosure, health-claim substantiation, fake reviews

### Takeaway
Under the FTC Act, affiliates and influencers must clearly and conspicuously disclose commissions, and any health claim must be backed by competent and reliable scientific evidence. For drug-like disease claims that generally means human clinical trials, which most RUO peptides lack. Endorsers themselves can be liable. The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (in effect since Oct 21, 2024) lets the FTC seek civil penalties of about $53,000 per violation for fake or misrepresented testimonials.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) were revised in 2023. Affiliate commissions are a "material connection" that must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. — [FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking); [FTC Disclosures 101 for Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf); [FTC Endorsements hub](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews)
- **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465):** announced Aug 2024, effective **Oct 21, 2024**. It bans fake or bought reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and fake social-influence indicators. Influencers can be liable under §465.2(a) if they misrepresent having used a product or their experience with it, and under §465.8 for fake influence metrics. The maximum penalty was $51,744 per violation, and 2025 FTC letters cite $53,088. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [FTC press release Aug 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [FTC 2025 warning-letter template](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-Fake-Review-Warning-Template.pdf)
- **Dec 2025:** The FTC sent warning letters to 10 companies about possible violations of the review rule, the first visible enforcement under it. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule); [FTC blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/12/warning-letter-or-ten-businesses-comply-ftcs-consumer-review-rule)
- **Teami precedent (Mar 2020):** The FTC settled with a tea marketer over unsupported weight-loss and health claims backed by paid influencers (a $15.2M judgment, mostly suspended), and sent warning letters to 10 of those influencers, including Cardi B and Jordin Sparks. One problem was disclosures hidden behind "more." — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/03/tea-marketer-misled-consumers-didnt-adequately-disclose-payments-well-known-influencers-ftc-alleges); [FTC blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2020/03/ftcs-teami-case-spilling-tea-about-influencers-advertisers); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/ftc-puts-influencers-and-sponsors-on-notice-with-dollar152-million-judgment.html)
- Law-firm commentary (Arnold & Porter, Dec 2025) describes increased FTC scrutiny of influencer marketing. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2025/12/influencer-marketing-under-the-microscope). Claims elsewhere of a "40% increase in enforcement" come from marketing blogs and are **unverified**.

### Inferences
- An affiliate saying a peptide "heals injuries" or "melts fat" makes a disease or structure claim it almost certainly cannot substantiate. That creates FTC deception exposure, separate from the FDA issues.
- Personal "I used it and it fixed my knee" testimonials are risky twice over: they are evidence of intended human use for FDA purposes, and if exaggerated or not genuine they can violate the review rule.

### Gaps
- I found no FTC action specifically about peptides.

## 5. Can affiliates be held personally liable?

### Takeaway
Yes, in principle, on several theories:
- FTC Act liability for their own deceptive claims, since endorsers can be directly liable.
- FDCA liability. Its prohibited acts cover "causing" the introduction of misbranded drugs, and misdemeanor liability does not require intent. Aiding and abetting and conspiracy charges also apply, as the Utah indictment shows.
- State consumer-protection laws.
- Civil suits from drug makers like Lilly.

In practice, enforcement so far has hit vendors, clinics and physicians, and I found no reported case against a pure affiliate. The risk rises sharply when affiliates give dosing or injection guidance, make health claims, promote GLP-1s or retatrutide, or work so closely with the vendor that they look like part of its marketing operation. **[LAWYER]** An FDA/FTC regulatory attorney should assess any specific business model.

### Cited Findings
- Under FTC Act Section 5, brands are responsible for claims made by their affiliates and influencers, and affiliates' deceptive claims also create exposure for themselves. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2025/12/influencer-marketing-under-the-microscope); [FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) (the Guides say endorsers can be liable for statements they make)
- The Utah peptide indictment includes **aiding and abetting** counts under the FDCA misbranding provisions. — [DOJ USAO-UT](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china)
- The Piper case was charged as a **conspiracy** to introduce misbranded drugs. — [DOJ USAO-WDMI](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmi/pr/2026_0723_Piper_Sentencing_PR)
- Health-law firms warn that referring patients or consumers to unapproved peptide sites can be treated as aiding and abetting distribution, and that influencer content describing human use undercuts RUO positioning. — [DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/can-you-use-influencers-to-promote-peptides/); [DJ Holt Law FAQ](https://djholtlaw.com/peptide-legal-frequently-asked-questions/)
- FDA has warned an affiliate-network site for unapproved drugs. — [FDA: PharmacyAffiliates.org](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/pharmacyaffiliatesorg-08162018)
- The FDCA's general penalty provision (21 U.S.C. §333) makes prohibited acts a misdemeanor punishable by up to 1 year. With intent to defraud or mislead, or for a repeat offense, it becomes a felony punishable by up to 3 years. — [21 U.S.C. §333 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/333) (statutory; standard FDCA framework)

### Inferences
- **Lower-exposure posture (still not risk-free):** promote only FDA-approved products, or licensed telehealth or pharmacy services that require a prescription. Make no efficacy, dosing or "sameness" claims. Disclose commissions clearly. Avoid RUO vendors entirely.
- **High-exposure posture:** RUO vendor links combined with "how to dose/reconstitute/inject" content, before-and-after testimonials, GLP-1 or retatrutide promotion, discount codes tied to human-use framing, or an affiliate who handles orders or products.
- Affiliate agreements often include indemnity clauses that push liability onto the affiliate. **[LAWYER]** to review contracts.

### Gaps
- No court decision found that addresses an affiliate's FDCA liability for peptide promotion specifically.

## 6. Platform, payment and affiliate-network restrictions

### Takeaway
Every major channel bans or heavily restricts this category:
- **Ads:** Google's Unapproved Substances and Healthcare policies, and Meta's drugs policy, prohibit promoting unapproved peptides.
- **Marketplaces:** Amazon purged many BPC-157 and TB-500 listings in late 2025.
- **Payments:** Stripe, PayPal and Shopify Payments prohibit or routinely terminate peptide and research-chemical merchants, often freezing funds.

Organic social content and affiliate links get removed regularly, partly through drug makers' mass reporting.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Ads:** The Unapproved Substances policy prohibits promoting non-government-approved products marketed as safe or effective for preventing, curing or treating disease. Google's Healthcare and medicines policy requires certification for prescription-drug promotion. — [Google: Unapproved substances](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595718?hl=en); [Google: Unapproved pharmaceuticals and supplements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/2423645?hl=en); [Google: Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Google Merchant Center/Shopping has a parallel policy. — [Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6165956). Agency guides say peptide names like BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin trigger disapproval. — [promarketer.ca](https://www.promarketer.ca/post/google-ads-for-peptides) (agency; illustrative)
- **Meta:** The Drugs & Pharmaceuticals ad standard restricts prescription drugs and prohibits ads for unsafe or unapproved substances. — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/)
- **TikTok/YouTube:** I didn't retrieve primary policy pages. Agency commentary says TikTok also bans these accounts. — [harborpointmarketingservices.com](https://www.harborpointmarketingservices.com/blog/how-to-advertise-peptides-without-getting-banned) (low reliability)
- **Amazon:** In December 2025, Amazon reportedly removed many BPC-157, AOD-9604, TB-500, MOTS-c, tesamorelin and sermorelin listings after having more than 100 BPC-157 listings in September, though some listings remained. — [BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/worlds-largest-retailers-selling-unapproved-peptides-and-performance-enhancing-drugs)
- **Payments:** Stripe's prohibited and restricted businesses list covers unapproved pharmaceuticals and products associated with regulatory gray areas. PayPal's Acceptable Use Policy prohibits prescription drugs. Shopify's AUP and Shopify Payments restrict research chemicals and pseudo-pharmaceuticals. Accounts are reportedly closed without warning and funds held for up to 180 days. — [InclusivePay](https://inclusivepay.com/peptide-payment-processor-shutdown/); [TechHikers](https://techhikers.com/shopify-banning-peptide-stores/) (**high-risk merchant processors with an interest in the topic; verify against the policies on stripe.com, paypal.com and shopify.com**)
- **Lilly's platform takedowns:** more than 14,000 websites, ads, posts and listings reported. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/g-s1-138594/eli-lilly-files-6-lawsuits-against-businesses-selling-black-market-obesity-drug)

### Inferences
- Peptide affiliate programs usually run in-house or on niche networks because mainstream networks won't host them. Platform bans also mean payouts and traffic are fragile.

## 7. Tax and business basics (brief)

### Takeaway
Affiliate commissions are self-employment income. They are reportable whether or not you receive a 1099, they are subject to income tax plus self-employment tax (about 15.3%), and quarterly estimated payments are typically required. Payers issue Form 1099-NEC above a reporting threshold, which I believe changed for 2026 payments but couldn't verify in this session.

### Cited Findings
- None verified in this session.

### Inferences
- An LLC alone does not shield someone from personal liability for their own deceptive statements or FDCA violations. **[LAWYER]** and a CPA are needed.

### Gaps
- The **1099-NEC threshold for 2026 is unverified.** It was $600 historically. My understanding, not verified here, is that the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised it to $2,000 for payments made after Dec 31, 2025, and restored the 1099-K threshold to $20,000 and 200 transactions. **Confirm with IRS.gov.**
