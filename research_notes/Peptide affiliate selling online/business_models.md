# Business Models for Selling Peptides Online (US focus, as of Oct 2026)

Method note: Research date 2026-10-06. Most primary article pages (Fortune, Chainalysis, AJMC, Public Citizen, Celine Gounder Substack, vendor sites) were blocked from full-text fetch by the network proxy, so many findings below rest on search-engine summaries/snippets of those pages rather than full reads. Treat exact figures as "reported by [source] per search summary". Where a source is a vendor, a marketing site, or a forum, it is flagged as anecdotal/self-interested.

Three legally distinct product categories run through all of this:
- **(a) "Research peptides" / RUO**: BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu injectable, "research" semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide vials. Sold labeled "for research use only, not for human consumption". The FDA treats this as selling unapproved new drugs when the obvious intent is human use (see enforcement below). Highest margins, highest legal and payment risk.
- **(b) Prescription peptides via telehealth/compounding**: compounded GLP-1s, sermorelin, etc., prescribed by licensed clinicians and filled by 503A/503B pharmacies. Legal if structured correctly, but under heavy regulatory change in 2026.
- **(c) OTC cosmetic/supplement peptides**: collagen peptide powders, topical copper peptide (GHK-Cu) serums, peptide skincare. Fully legal mainstream consumer goods (Amazon, retail, DTC). Lowest risk, lowest margins.

## 1. What are the main business models, and how does each work?

### Takeaway
There are roughly seven models: (1) affiliate/creator discount codes for RUO vendors, (2) affiliate/CPA for telehealth GLP-1/peptide clinics, (3) own-brand RUO store sourcing from Chinese manufacturers (white-label/private-label or reseller), (4) white-label telehealth brand built on infrastructure like OpenLoop, (5) brick-and-mortar or virtual peptide clinic / med spa / coach models, (6) paid communities, courses and "group buys", and (7) mainstream OTC collagen/copper-peptide affiliate or private-label. The money and the risk both concentrate in categories (a) and (b).

### Cited Findings

**Model 1: Affiliate / creator codes for research-peptide (RUO) vendors (category a)**
- Typical RUO vendor affiliate terms listed on vendor pages (self-reported, vendor-owned): Spartan Peptides 10%; Improved Peptides 20% first order + 10% recurring; LiveWell Peptides up to 22%; 99 Purity Peptides 10% rising to 25%; CertaPeptides 15-25%; True Peptides 10-20%; Midwest Peptide 10%; Evolve Peptides 15% — [Spartan](https://spartanpeptides.com/spartan-affiliate/), [Improved](https://improvedpeptides.com/affiliate-program/), [LiveWell](https://www.livewellpeptides.com/affiliate-program/), [99 Purity](https://99puritypeptides.com/affiliates), [Certa](https://certapeptides.com/affiliate), [True](https://true-peptides.com/affiliate), [Midwest](https://midwestpeptide.com/affiliates), [Evolve](https://www.evolvepeptides.com/peptide-affiliate-program/). A vendor-run comparison ("Best Peptide Affiliate Program in 2026: 7 Programs Compared by What They Actually Pay") also exists — [ValidatedPeptides](https://validatedpeptides.com/research/best-peptide-affiliate-program) (vendor source; could not be fetched).
- How it works in practice: creators say they are "not sponsored" but link to a peptide seller with a discount code that earns them a cut of each sale; they use code words such as "Peppers," "Pep," or "the pepper community" to avoid moderation — [Celine Gounder, "The people selling you peptides on TikTok are making money off your trust"](https://www.celinegounder.com/p/peptides-tiktok-affiliates-misinformation) (per search summary; physician/journalist commentary).
- Gray-market peptide sellers are promoted on TikTok via affiliates and discount codes while pharmacists warn of safety risks — [TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/gray-market-peptides-flood-tiktok-as-pharmacists-warn-of-safety-risks); a "disguised research code" style of promotion is documented in a video teardown — [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/videos/peptides/disguised-research-code-cale-also-20-off-today-btw).

**Model 2: Telehealth GLP-1 / peptide affiliate (CPA/CPS) programs (category b)**
- Reported payouts for GLP-1 telehealth affiliate programs range from about $35 to $500 per acquisition — [AffRoom](https://affroom.com/blog/best-ozempic-affiliate-programs/) (affiliate-industry blog).
- Example per-sale payouts listed on an affiliate network (Aug-Sep 2026): TrimRX $187.50; Gala Tirzepatide $352; RetaClinic $280; BMI MD $280; RightWell $270 — [WOWtrk offers](https://www.wowtrk.com/offer/2035752) (affiliate network listings; summarized by search engine, could not verify each page individually). Some telehealth brands also run programs on mainstream networks such as Awin — [Awin merchant profile](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/120772). Weight-loss affiliate monetization is marketed by networks such as Katalys — [Katalys](https://katalys.com/industries/weight-loss-affiliate-programs/).
- Older (2023) Fortune investigation: weight-loss content on TikTok/YouTube/Instagram is underpinned by "a web of financial incentives," with influencers earning referral points via affiliate codes that translate into discounts on their own medication — [Fortune, Sept 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/09/19/inside-ozempic-tiktok-weight-loss-influencers-hidden-economy-telehealth-cash-drug-discounts-mounjaro-glp1-wegovy/) (OLDER: 2023).
- Regulatory pressure on this model: FDA sent warning letters to 30 telehealth companies on Feb 20, 2026 over website claims about compounded semaglutide/tirzepatide; this followed HHS General Counsel's Feb 6, 2026 referral of Hims & Hers to DOJ — [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/fda-warns-telehealth-companies-over-marketing-of-compounded-glp-1-weight-loss-drugs/), [Drug Topics](https://www.drugtopics.com/view/fda-issues-warnings-to-30-telehealth-companies-against-illegal-marketing-of-compounded-glp-1-medications). Law firm guidance on compounded GLP-1 advertising rules — [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/compounded-glp-1-advertising-what-compounders-need-to-know-now). Telehealth companies have also been sued over corporate-practice-of-medicine violations — [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/telehealth-companies-sued-for-selling-compounded-glp-1-drugs-and-violations-of-corporate-practice-of-medicine-laws.html).
- Many online GLP-1 sellers skip clinician oversight — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/online-glp-1-sellers-often-skip-clinician-oversight).

**Model 3: Own-brand RUO store (white-label/private-label or reseller of Chinese-made powder) (category a)**
- Economics: a milligram of BPC-157 that costs under $1 to synthesize without quality controls can retail for $30-$80, with no regulatory compliance, clinical testing or liability costs — [RethinkPeptides, "The Research Use Only Fiction"](https://rethinkpeptides.com/articles/for-research-use-only-the-legal-fiction-of-gray-market-peptides) (per search summary; not independently verified).
- Chinese suppliers received $41.4M in crypto in 2025 (Chainalysis); on-chain data indicate some gray-market vendors are Chinese chemical makers that pivoted from fentanyl/amphetamine precursors — [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/gray-market-peptide-crypto-boom/) (via search summary), [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/crypto-payments-to-gray-market-peptide-vendors-surge-700-in-2026).
- Chinese B2B listings advertise custom peptides, COAs per batch, and in some cases no minimum order quantity — [Global Sources listing](https://exhibitions.globalsources.com/products/HK/fm2-full-range-of-specifications-customized-peptides-on-demand_1237118821/).
- Bloomberg Odd Lots episode "The Booming Business of Chinese Peptides" (Dec 19, 2025) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2025-12-19/chinese-peptides-are-a-booming-business-in-the-us).
- Some US "wholesale programs" sell finished, tested inventory to would-be resellers, positioned as an alternative to sourcing synthesis yourself — [RealPeptides "cost to start a peptide company"](https://www.realpeptides.co/cost-to-start-a-peptide-company/) (vendor source).

**Model 4: White-label telehealth brand (category b)**
- OpenLoop offers white-label telehealth: licensed clinicians in all 50 states, compliant workflows, integrated pharmacy fulfillment, EHR, payments; "you own the brand and the patient relationship"; programs include GLP-1s, peptides, hormones; marketing claims launch "in as little as a day" and that no medical background is needed — [OpenLoop](https://openloophealth.com/?p=573), [OpenLoop demo page](https://go.openloophealth.com/?p=75). No public pricing found.
- Bloomberg (2026) describes a "billion-dollar peptides gold rush" as legalization approaches, with entrepreneurs, investors and healthcare players racing to cash in; patients used lenient telehealth providers and longevity clinics paying cash — [Bloomberg, "Peptides Fuel a Multibillion-Dollar Startup Race"](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-peptide-legalization-gold-rush/) (paywalled; per search summary).
- Scale benchmark: Hims & Hers 2025 revenue $2.35B (vs $1.5B 2024), net income ~$128M, 2.5M subscribers, ~$83 monthly revenue per subscriber; targeted ~$725M weight-loss revenue for 2025 — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hims-hers-bullish-weight-loss-business-despite-glp-1-market-shift), [Fintool Q4 2025](https://fintool.com/app/research/companies/HIMS/earnings/Q4%202025) (figures per search summary; check against filings). Hims acquired a California peptide manufacturing facility in 2024 — [Bloomberg feature, per search summary](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-peptide-legalization-gold-rush/).

**Model 5: Peptide clinic / med spa / coach (category b, sometimes bleeding into a)**
- A financial-model vendor estimates a physical peptide therapy clinic needs ~$746K minimum cash by month 6 (incl. $36.55K capex, $35K initial inventory) — [Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/startup-costs/peptide-therapy) (template seller; assumptions, not observed data; some line items such as $125K/month lease look implausibly high).
- Peptide certification courses are sold to medical professionals, estheticians, wellness coaches and non-medical practitioners — [ABAC Training](https://abactraining.com/peptides-mini-course/).

**Model 6: Paid communities, courses, "group buys" (info products; touches a)**
- Skool peptide communities (figures from a third-party Skool directory, ANECDOTAL/unverified): Coach Cam's Peptide Academy ~1,571 members at $10/mo (~$15.6K); "The Grey Lab," a peptide group-buy community, 713 members at $25/mo (~$15.1K MRR); Hacksmith's Peptalk ~3,034 members at $10/mo with a 2,400-member free feeder group; "Reta Unfiltered Inner Circle" 3,500+ members; free "Peptide of the Week" community ~13,870 members — [Skoolmakers directory](https://skoolmakers.com/communities/), [Peptide Coaching Hub](https://www.skool.com/peptide-coaching-hub-6999/about).
- Group buys (members pooling orders direct from Chinese manufacturers, often organized via Telegram) are listed in gray-market sourcing guides — [Gray.guide](https://www.gray.guide/sourcing-details) (community source, anecdotal). A pharmacy-industry newsletter has floated group-buying discounts on peptide APIs and testing — [503Pharma newsletter](https://503pharma-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/would-a-group-buying-discount-on-peptide-apis-and-testing-help-you).

**Model 7: OTC collagen / copper-peptide skincare (category c)**
- Collagen peptides market estimates disagree widely: ~$2.86B in 2025 rising to $7.77B by 2034 (one report) vs ~$699M in 2023 to ~$922M by 2028 (another); Technavio projects +$721.7M growth 2024-2029 at 8.5% CAGR — [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/collagen-peptide-market-industry-analysis), [Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/product/collagen-peptides-market-5932), [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/blog/FB/collagen-peptide-market-outlook) (which report gives which number was not fully clear from snippets; flag as conflicting).
- Copper peptide skincare market ~$285M in 2024, projected ~$612M by 2033 — [DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/copper-peptide-skincare-market-71167).
- Category (c) is monetized mainly through Amazon Associates/deal sites and DTC brands (e.g., deal posts for Vital Proteins, Physician's Choice collagen) — [Hip2Save](https://hip2save.com/deals/vital-proteins/), [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/19098721-physician-s-choice-collagen-peptides-hydrolyzed-protein-powder-for-hair-skin-joints-grass-fed-non-gmo-11-69).

### Inferences
- Affiliate commissions for RUO vendors (10-25% of sale) are percentage-based and recurring-friendly, while telehealth programs pay flat CPA bounties ($35-$500), reflecting multi-month subscriber lifetime value.
- "Dropshipping" in category (a) shows up mostly as resellers buying tested finished vials from US wholesalers or labeling Chinese powder; I found no credible source documenting classic Shopify-style dropshipping of RUO peptides, likely because Shopify/Stripe ban it.
- Category (c) is a normal low-margin affiliate niche; the "peptide boom" money and the platform workarounds are almost entirely categories (a) and (b).

### Gaps
- No verified, journalist-sourced earnings for individual peptide affiliates or creators were found. Do not cite specific influencer income.
- Amazon Associates commission rate for supplements/beauty was not confirmed in this session.
- OpenLoop and similar white-label telehealth pricing is not public.

## 2. How do sellers acquire customers, and what workarounds do they use given platform bans?

### Takeaway
Acquisition runs through TikTok/Instagram/YouTube creators with discount codes, podcasts, Skool/Telegram/Discord communities and SEO "research" blogs. Workarounds include coded language ("peppers"), "not sponsored" framing, "research use only" labels, moving payments to crypto and high-risk processors, and moving conversations into closed communities.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok creators use code words ("Peppers," "Pep," "the pepper community") to dodge moderation and link to sellers with discount codes while claiming not to be sponsored — [Celine Gounder](https://www.celinegounder.com/p/peptides-tiktok-affiliates-misinformation) (per search summary).
- Influencer GLP-1 content often fails to disclose sponsorship and overstates benefits — [Axios, Feb 2026](https://www.axios.com/health/2026/02/09) (per search summary).
- Payments: Stripe, PayPal and Square ban peptide merchants; accounts that get flagged are reportedly frozen 90-180 days and placed on the MATCH list; a 2026 Mastercard BRAM update (GLB 11691.1) reportedly tightened enforcement on research peptides and nutraceuticals — [InclusivePay](https://inclusivepay.com/peptide-payment-processor-shutdown/) (high-risk processor marketing; self-interested source). High-risk gateway lists are marketed to peptide stores — [onPoint Studio](https://onpoint.to/10-peptide-store-payment-gateways/), [2Accept](https://www.2accept.net/blog/why-peptide-sellers-need-specialized-payment-solutions), [Instabill](https://instabill.com/?p=866).
- Crypto: Bloomberg reported (July 20, 2026) that crypto has become the default way to pay for research peptides online after two years of banks and card networks cutting sellers off — [Bloomberg via search summary](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-peptide-legalization-gold-rush/) (exact article URL not retrieved). Chainalysis: peptide vendors received $32M in crypto in Q1 2026, a ~700% year-on-year increase, projecting >$100M for 2026 — [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/gray-market-peptide-crypto-boom/), [Fortune, June 4, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/peptides-crypto-looksmaxxing-economy-chainalysis-report-100-million/), [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/crypto-rails-are-powering-a-100-million-peptide-gray-market/). Fortune ties this to the "looksmaxxing" online subculture.
- Closed communities: Telegram vendor/group-buy channels are catalogued by community guides — [Gray.guide](https://www.gray.guide/sourcing-details) (anecdotal). Skool hosts paid and free peptide communities, some with free "feeder" groups funnelling into paid tiers — [Skoolmakers](https://skoolmakers.com/communities/).
- SEO: vendor-owned "research" and comparison content is common, e.g., an SEO article explicitly titled as exploiting the 2026 FDA BPC-157 news ("Why this is MASSIVE SEO RIGHT NOW") — [RejuvJuice](https://www.rejuvjuice.com/post/bpc-157-banned-fda-peptide-controversy); vendor "state of research peptides 2026" content — [Kylo Peptides](https://kylopeptides.com/the-state-of-research-peptides-2026/).

### Inferences
- The typical funnel for category (a) is: short-form video (coded language) to link-in-bio or a community, then to a vendor site with a code, then payment by crypto/ACH/high-risk processor.
- Category (b) can use mainstream affiliate networks and paid ads, subject to FDA/FTC advertising rules, which were enforced more heavily in 2026.

### Gaps
- No specific platform policy texts (TikTok, Meta, YouTube) on peptides were retrieved this session.
- Telegram/Discord scale (member counts, revenue) was not documented by credible journalism found here.

## 3. What do credible sources say about money made and the 2024-2026 boom?

### Takeaway
Credible quantification is thin but consistent: Chainalysis measured ~$32M in Q1 2026 crypto flows to peptide vendors (+700% YoY), with $41.4M to Chinese suppliers in 2025. Bloomberg calls it a "multibillion-dollar" startup race tied to expected legalization. 2026 brought both a crackdown (FDA warning letters, Lilly lawsuits, a criminal case) and a partial deregulation push (FDA advisory committee votes in July 2026). Hims & Hers shows the scale of the legal telehealth side.

### Cited Findings
- Chainalysis/Fortune crypto figures as above — [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/gray-market-peptide-crypto-boom/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/peptides-crypto-looksmaxxing-economy-chainalysis-report-100-million/). These measure crypto payments only, not total sales (card, ACH, Zelle not counted).
- Quality: lab tests of seized gray-market peptides reportedly found 30% had the wrong ingredient, two-thirds failed basic purity standards, and some had arsenic at 10x safety limits — [KuCoin/Chainalysis-related coverage via search summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/crypto-payments-to-gray-market-peptide-vendors-surge-700-in-2026) (original lab study not identified; flag).
- Bloomberg 2026 feature on a "billion-dollar peptides gold rush" — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-peptide-legalization-gold-rush/); Bloomberg on NAD and peptides as "anti-aging injection of the moment" (Sept 2025) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-19/do-nad-and-peptides-work-the-anti-aging-injection-of-the-moment).
- STAT/Undark investigation on BPC-157's big claims and scant evidence (Feb 3, 2026) — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/02/03/bpc-157-peptide-science-safety-regulatory-questions/); STAT on RFK Jr. backing peptides (Apr 6, 2026) — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/04/06/rfk-jr-apparent-contradiction-peptides-vaccines-medical-libertarianism/).
- Enforcement against RUO sellers: since Dec 2024 the FDA issued 14 warning letters to retatrutide sellers, and as of May 2026, 11 of 14 still advertised unapproved peptides; Eli Lilly filed six federal suits and referred 200+ entities to regulators; federal prosecutors charged Paradigm Peptides (criminal information, Oct 2025) and a Florida man for selling retatrutide, while 100+ companies openly sell it — [InsideHealthPolicy](https://insidehealthpolicy.com/daily-news/fda-steps-retatrutide-enforcement-against-online-peptide-sellers-following-eli-lilly-suit), [AMC Defense Law](https://www.amcdefenselaw.com/justice-watch/eli-lilly-retatrutide-lawsuits-peptide-sellers-2026), [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/the-miracle-drug-and-the-peptide-craze-breaking-the-fda/) (per search summaries). FDA warning letter to Gram Peptides, Mar 31, 2026 — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026). Five more warning letters (Peak Performance Peptides, Royal Peptides, NuScience Peptides, Peptide Partners, Tex Peptides) said RUO labeling "is not a legal strategy" — [Arent Fox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy).
- Deregulation track: FDA signalled intent to remove 12 peptides from the 503A Category 2 ("significant safety concerns") list; the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee met July 23-24, 2026 and voted 8-6 (1 abstention) to support compounding BPC-157 (for ulcerative colitis); full Category 1 placement needs notice-and-comment rulemaking, estimated 8-12 months — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2026/05/fda-signals-potentially-evolving-stance-toward-compounding-of-certain-peptides), [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fda-signals-potentially-evolving-stance-5479727/), [PharmExec, July 24, 2026](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-votes-loosen-restrictions-four-peptides), [HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/fda-panel-votes-bpc-157-compounding/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/fda-panel-to-revisit-biden-peptide-ban-as-it-weighs-looser-rules). One report says the 14-member panel included six peptide sellers — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/peptide-regulation-debate-intensifies-after-fda-advisory-panel-s-controversial-vote) (per search summary; verify). Sources conflict on count: PharmExec headline says "four peptides" loosened; another summary says "six peptides including BPC-157."
- NYT and WSJ reported the FDA was expected to lift restrictions so compounding pharmacies could sell certain peptides — [Sherwood News citing NYT](https://sherwood.news/markets/the-fda-is-expected-to-lift-restriction-on-certain-peptides-the-nyt-reports/).
- Forbes (July 21, 2026) frames peptides as a growing public-health challenge — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/omerawan/2026/07/21/fdas-review-of-peptides-signals-a-growing-public-health-challenge--separating-science-from-hype/).
- Telehealth benchmark: Hims & Hers 2025 revenue $2.35B, ~2.5M subscribers — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hims-hers-bullish-weight-loss-business-despite-glp-1-market-shift).

### Inferences
- If BPC-157 and others move to Category 1, revenue is likely to shift from the RUO gray market toward prescription telehealth/compounding (category b), which is what Bloomberg's "startup race" describes. This is a forecast, not a confirmed outcome.
- Enforcement so far is sparse relative to the number of sellers (14 letters vs 100+ open retatrutide sellers), which explains why the RUO model persists. Legal exposure (criminal charges, Lilly suits) is real, though.

### Gaps
- No credible total US market size for RUO peptides found; Chainalysis covers crypto only.
- Did not retrieve full NYT/WSJ/Wired investigations. Wired coverage specifically was not found.
- Final FDA action after the July 2026 votes (as of Oct 2026) was not confirmed.

## 4. Realistic startup costs and operational requirements

### Takeaway
Credible, specific startup-cost data is scarce. Category (a) needs inventory (Chinese powder or US wholesale vials), per-batch third-party testing (HPLC/MS identity and purity, ideally endotoxin/heavy metals), a high-risk merchant account or crypto, and legal exposure budgeting. Category (b) via white-label telehealth needs no clinical license but does need compliance with FDA/FTC advertising and state corporate-practice-of-medicine rules. A physical clinic is a six-figure undertaking. Category (c) affiliate needs only content.

### Cited Findings
- Cost depends on whether you contract synthesis and testing yourself or buy finished, independently tested inventory via a wholesale program — [RealPeptides](https://www.realpeptides.co/cost-to-start-a-peptide-company/) (vendor source).
- Testing scope for RUO peptides offered by contract labs: identity, purity, potency, microbial, heavy metals, endotoxin — [ContractLaboratory](https://contractlaboratory.com/?p=231952). Vendors commonly cite Janoshik Analytics as an independent tester — [Stonevillenc blog](https://stonevillenc.org/chinese-peptids-focus/) (low-quality SEO source; Janoshik pricing not found).
- Chinese suppliers advertise per-batch COAs; some have no MOQ while many wholesalers require high MOQs — [Global Sources](https://exhibitions.globalsources.com/products/HK/fm2-full-range-of-specifications-customized-peptides-on-demand_1237118821/), [Stonevillenc](https://stonevillenc.org/china-bulk-peptides-today/) (low-quality source).
- Merchant accounts: mainstream processors ban peptides; frozen funds 90-180 days and MATCH listing are reported risks — [InclusivePay](https://inclusivepay.com/peptide-payment-processor-shutdown/) (self-interested). Shopify community discussion of Stripe's restricted list — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/stripe-restricted-businesses-list-you-gotta-see-this-list/167773).
- Scam/raid risk in the vendor ecosystem is high enough that "fake vendor" lists circulate — [Nootroholic](https://nootroholic.com/peptide-scams-to-avoid) (anecdotal).
- Clinic model: ~$746K minimum cash by month 6 (template estimate) — [Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/startup-costs/peptide-therapy) (model assumptions, treat skeptically).
- White-label telehealth: OpenLoop claims launch in about a day with no medical background; pricing undisclosed — [OpenLoop](https://openloophealth.com/?p=573).
- Legal defense firms now advertise "Peptide & GLP-1 fraud defense," a sign of rising prosecution risk — [Armstrong Brady Lyons](https://www.armstrongbradylyons.com/peptide-glp1-fraud-defense).

### Inferences
- Affiliate models (RUO codes, telehealth CPA, Amazon collagen) need almost no capital. The cost is audience-building, plus legal/ethical exposure for RUO promotion (FTC disclosure rules apply; "not sponsored" claims with affiliate codes are a likely FTC Endorsement Guides problem).
- An own-brand RUO store needs inventory plus testing plus a site plus high-risk payments. Credible dollar ranges were not found, so do not state a number.

### Gaps
- No credible figures for typical RUO store startup costs, Janoshik/third-party test pricing, or high-risk processor fees (rates/reserves).
- No verified margin data beyond the BPC-157 "<$1/mg cost vs $30-80 retail" claim (single, non-primary source).
- Non-US notes: not researched in depth. Chainalysis indicates heavy China supply-side involvement; nothing gathered on UK/EU/Australia rules.
