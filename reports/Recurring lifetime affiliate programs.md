# HighLevel leads a short list of lifetime payers

**Bottom line:** As of October 2026, only a handful of software affiliate programs still pay **25% or more, every month, for as long as the customer stays**, on products priced near or above $100/month. On the evidence gathered, the best fit for your "earn $100-150 per customer within 2-3 months" test is **HighLevel (40% for life, $97-$497/month plans)**, followed by **ClickFunnels (30% for life on a $197 plan)**, **Mighty Networks (30% for life on a $215 plan, though one official document says 20%)**, **Skool (40% for life on a $99 plan)** and **Thinkific (30% for life on a $109 plan)**. **SE Ranking** also meets the money test, but its terms appear to ban paid search ads altogether. **Kajabi no longer qualifies**: since May 2025 new partners get a one-time payment for their first four referrals and then only 10-20% recurring. **ManyChat pays 30-50% for 12 months only**, on a plan as cheap as $39/month, and **no source could be found for the "$600k/month ManyChat affiliate" story**. The arithmetic makes it very unlikely. Every program with published rules **bans bidding on its brand name** in Google Ads, so the workable approach is non-brand keywords sent to your own review or comparison page. **One important limit on this research:** the researchers' network blocked them from opening any official page directly. Every figure below comes either from search-engine excerpts of the company's own pages (marked **Official (snippet)**) or from third-party directories and blogs (marked **Third-party**). Open each program's official terms page and confirm the numbers before you spend anything on ads.

## The ranked shortlist: five programs pass the test, one more is blocked for ads

Customers on annual billing usually pay a lower effective monthly price, so they earn you less per month. All of these programs also hold commissions for roughly 30-60 days before paying them out, so the cash arrives a month or two after the customer pays.

HighLevel stands out because its prices are three to ten times higher than typical email or chat tools. HighLevel's own help article gives the example of a $297/month Unlimited customer paying the affiliate **$118.80 "every month they remain a customer"** ([HighLevel Support](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48000980325-become-a-highlevel-affiliate)), and lists plans at $97, $297 and $497 ([HighLevel Pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)). Its channel is very large: a HighLevel blog post title says it paid **$6M in affiliate commissions in August 2025** ([HighLevel blog](https://www.gohighlevel.com/post/6m-in-affiliate-commissions-paid-out-in-august)). A large channel also means heavy competition for the same buyers. One catch: commissions may be held until they pass $50 and **may be forfeited if $50 isn't reached within 120 days** ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)).

ClickFunnels pays 30% "for the life of the customer" with a 45-day cookie ([ClickFunnels blog](https://www.clickfunnels.com/blog/clickfunnels-2-0-affiliate-program/)) on plans of $97, $197 and $297 ([ClickFunnels Pricing](https://www.clickfunnels.com/pricing)). Mighty Networks advertises "30% lifetime commissions… no caps, no limits, no expiration" ([Mighty Partner Program](https://www.mightynetworks.com/partner-program)), but its affiliate agreement states 20% for Premium plans ([Mighty Affiliate Agreement](https://www.mightynetworks.com/affiliate-program-agreement)). Ask Mighty which rate applies to you before relying on it. Skool's help center describes 40% of the subscription, about $39 a month per Pro customer, with a short 14-day last-click cookie ([Skool Help](https://help.skool.com/article/33-how-to-refer-others-to-skool)). Thinkific's own page says "30% lifetime recurring commission on all monthly or annual standard paid plans" ([Thinkific Affiliates](https://www.thinkific.com/affiliates/)). A **roughly 10% price increase from August 13, 2026** brought the Start plan to $109 ([Thinkific pricing update](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/41582716364567-Thinkific-s-2026-Plan-Pricing-Update-What-It-Means-for-You)), which also raised the commission per customer.

SE Ranking's terms pay 30% on "all of the user's subsequent payments and rebills," and a referred user is "forever credited" to you ([SE Ranking affiliate terms](https://seranking.com/legal/affiliate.html); [SE Ranking FAQ](https://help.seranking.com/hc/en-us/articles/16332910001180-Affiliate-program-FAQ)). However, the same terms prohibit "any paid search placement including an advertisement that you purchased through bidding on keywords." Treat SE Ranking as unusable for Google Ads unless its affiliate manager approves your plan in writing.

## Close runners-up pay well but fail one test

A second group pays 25%+ for life but on cheaper plans, so each customer takes **4-6 months** to reach $100-150. **LearnWorlds** pays "up to 30% recurring… every month, for life" ([LearnWorlds Partners Club](https://www.learnworlds.com/resources/partners/partners-club/)). On the $99 Pro Trainer plan ([LearnWorlds Pricing](https://www.learnworlds.com/pricing/)) that is $29.70 a month. It bans brand bidding without prior approval ([LearnWorlds terms](https://www.learnworlds.com/affiliate-program-terms-conditions/)). **Leadpages** pays 30% recurring with a 90-day cookie and bans brand keywords ([Leadpages Support](https://support.leadpages.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009044771-Leadpages-Affiliate-Partner-Program)). Its plan prices were ambiguous in the excerpts (about $99 for Pro). **Systeme.io** shows "60% lifetime recurring commissions" in an excerpt of its official page ([Systeme.io Affiliate](https://systeme.io/affiliate-program)), up from the widely quoted 40%, so verify it. Its plans run only $27-$97 a month ([Systeme.io Pricing](https://systeme.io/pricing)). **Builderall** pays 30% recurring, but **halves your commissions if you make no direct sale for 3 months** ([Builderall Hub](https://hub.builderall.com/builderall/kb/builderall-affiliates/active-vs-inactive-affiliate)). **StreamYard** pays 25% "as long as the person remains subscribed" ([StreamYard Affiliate Terms](https://support.streamyard.com/hc/en-us/articles/31889400259092-Affiliate-Terms)), which is $74.75 a month on its $299 Business plan (price Third-party). It bans PPC without prior written permission. Email tools **AWeber (30-50%, paid "every time a referred customer pays")** ([AWeber Advocates](https://www.aweber.com/advocates.htm)) and **Moosend (30-40% while the referral pays)** ([Moosend Affiliate](https://moosend.com/affiliate-program/)) pay for life, but their plan prices were not confirmed, so no per-customer estimate is given.

A third group reaches $100-150 fast but **stops paying after a fixed period**. That suits paid ads, but it does not build lasting income. **SamCart** pays 30% "for up to 2 years" ([SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/affiliate-sign-up)): $47.70 a month on the $159 Grow plan, which reaches $100-150 in about 2-3 months, capped at about $1,145 per customer. **Kartra** pays up to 40%, but only for 12 months and only at its top tier (over $10,000 in sales); its page states there are no lifetime commissions ([Kartra Partners](https://kartra.com/partners/)). **Teachable** pays 30% "for a full year" ([Teachable Partners](https://www.teachable.com/partners)), about $26.70 a month on the $89 Builder plan, so at most about $320 per customer. **Webflow** pays 50% for up to 12 months ([Webflow Affiliates](https://webflow.com/solutions/affiliates)). **Kit** pays 50% for the first year, then 10-20% only for affiliates who keep a status tier ([Kit Affiliate](https://kit.com/affiliate)). **Simplero** pays 20% for 18 months ([Simplero Partner](https://simplero.com/partner)). Programs below the bar include Circle ($100 upfront plus 10-20% recurring) ([Circle](https://circle.so/affiliate-program)), Podia (20%, 12 months) ([Podia Help](https://help.podia.com/en/articles/11370941-becoming-a-podia-affiliate)), Stan Store (20%, and you must keep your own Stan subscription) ([Stan Help](https://help.stan.store/article/89-what-is-stans-referral-or-affiliate-program)), and hosting programs such as Kinsta (10% for life) and Cloudways (7% for life). Many large brands, including Semrush, Shopify, Wix and Housecall Pro, pay **one-time bounties only**, and Ahrefs and Zapier have no public affiliate program.

## Kajabi and ManyChat no longer fit the model

**Kajabi changed its program in 2025.** A March 2025 Kajabi update urged partners to lock in "30% Partner commissions before tiered commissions begin in May" ([Kajabi Updates](https://kajabi.com/updates/join-our-partner-program)). Under the new structure, your **first four referrals pay a one-time 100% of their first month**. After that, recurring commission is **10% at 5-24 active referrals, 15% at 25-49 and 20% at 50+**, paid while you and the referral stay active ([Kajabi Help: Partner Program overview](https://help.kajabi.com/articles/resources/kajabi-partner-program/partner-program-overview)). Partners who joined before the change keep 30% only conditionally. They **lose it if they go 12 months without a new referral**, or if they cancel their Kajabi subscription and switch to the standalone partner program ([Kajabi Partner Terms FAQ](https://help.kajabi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360046907554-Updated-Partner-Terms-Services-FAQ-s)). On the $149 Basic plan, 10% is just $14.90 a month, so one customer takes **7-10 months** to reach $100-150. Kajabi's ad rules are also strict. You may not use "Kajabi" in any keyword (for example, "Kajabi courses"), may not link ads directly to Kajabi, and may not put your partner link in the ad itself. You may run ads for generic terms like "online course builder" to your own page ([Kajabi Partner Terms FAQs](https://help.kajabi.com/en/articles/17175741-partner-terms-and-services-faqs)).

**ManyChat pays well for a year, then stops.** Its official terms and FAQ set three tiers based on how many paid signups you have referred in total: **30% (0-30 signups), 40% (31-200) and 50% (201+), all for 12 months only** ([ManyChat Affiliate Terms](https://manychat.com/legal/affiliate-program-terms); [ManyChat Partner FAQ](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/20518248843164-Manychat-Partner-Program-FAQ)). The Pro plan now costs **$39 a month for up to 2,500 active contacts** ($29 billed annually), with extra charges for more contacts ([ManyChat Help: Pro plan](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/25800228332572-Pro-plan)). At 30%, that is $11.70 a month: about 9 months to reach $100, and the 12-month cap stops it at $140.40, short of $150. Larger accounts pay more. The official terms say the cookie lasts 90 days, while third-party directories still list 120 days ([Reditus](https://getreditus.com/affiliate-programs/manychat)). Payouts go through PartnerStack. For example, a customer payment on April 15 is approved May 15 and paid to you on June 13. ManyChat bans brand-term bidding without written permission.

**The "$600k/month" ManyChat story could not be verified.** Searches found no case study, payout proof or official statement behind it ([search results incl. Reditus](https://getreditus.com/affiliate-programs/manychat)). The researchers did not find where the story came from, so they could not check its original source. The arithmetic also argues against it. At the top 50% rate on a $39 plan, each customer pays $19.50 a month, so $600,000 a month would need **about 30,800 paying referrals at once**. Because commissions stop after a year, every one of them would have to have signed up within the previous 12 months. Even if the average account paid $100 a month, it would take about 12,000. The story more likely mixes up something else, such as total payouts across all ManyChat affiliates or someone's own business that uses ManyChat to sell its own products. Treat it as unproven marketing.

## Google Ads can pay, but only with a real page and a long-staying customer

**The brand-name ban is nearly universal.** HighLevel, ManyChat, ActiveCampaign, Kajabi, ClickFunnels, Mighty, Circle, LearnWorlds, Kartra, Leadpages, Systeme.io and Webflow all prohibit bidding on their brand names in their official terms ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms); [ClickFunnels Affiliate Agreement](https://app.clickfunnels.com/affiliate-agreement); [Mighty T&Cs](https://affiliate.mightynetworks.com/terms)). Google's own policy does not restrict trademarked keywords, but the program's terms do, and the stricter rule wins ([BrandVerity](https://www.brandverity.com/blog/google-adwords-trademark-policies-top-8-questions)). Keywords like "X vs Y" or "X alternative" contain brand names, so they likely break these rules too. ClickFunnels goes further and also bans bidding on competitors' trademarks. GetResponse bans paid ads entirely without written consent ([GetResponse Affiliate Terms](https://www.getresponse.com/legal/affiliate-terms-of-service)). Breaking these rules typically costs you your commissions and your place in the program. Mighty sets your balance to $0 without warning.

**Google also rejects thin pages.** A landing page that exists only to forward visitors to the vendor can be disapproved as "insufficient original content." Implying you *are* the brand can lead to an account suspension with no warning, and you may not be allowed to advertise with Google again ([Google Ads policy, per search excerpt](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938071); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ads-updates-destination-requirements-policy/439612/)). The safe setup is a genuine review or comparison page on your own website, such as "best CRM for agencies," reached through non-brand keywords and containing your affiliate links.

**The cost per paying customer is high.** WordStream's 2025 benchmarks put the average Google Ads cost per click (CPC) at **$5.26 across all industries** ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/resources/google-ads-industry-benchmarks)). Agencies report **$6-$12** for mid-intent software keywords and $15+ for high-intent buying terms ([Powered by Search](https://www.poweredbysearch.com/learn/b2b-saas-google-ads-stats-benchmarks/)). Free trials convert to paid at **about 18-25% when no credit card is required** and about 49-60% when one is ([Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026)). The researchers built an illustrative funnel from assumptions, not measured data: 30% of visitors click through to the vendor, 10% of those start a trial, and 25% of trials become paying. In that funnel, about 0.75% of ad clicks become a paying customer. At $6-$10 per click, **each paying customer would cost about $800-$1,300 in ads**. This changes how to read your test. Earning $100-150 in 2-3 months is a good sign of fast payback, but it does not mean you break even. You break even only if the customer keeps paying long enough to cover what you spent to get them.

**How long customers stay decides the result.** Small-business software typically loses **about 3-7% of customers a month**: roughly 6.1% for cheap plans under $25 and 2.2% for accounts over $500 ([Churn Buster](https://churnbuster.io/articles/b2b-saas-churn-rate/)). As a rough rule, a customer stays for about 1 divided by the monthly loss rate. That works out to about 20 months at 5%, so a "lifetime" commission usually pays for **14-33 months, not forever**. The notes' worked example is a $97 plan at 40%, which pays $38.80 a month and totals about **$776 at 5% monthly churn**. That sits at the low end of the $800-$1,300 ad cost estimate. Applying the same rule to HighLevel's $297 plan gives about $2,376 ($118.80 for about 20 months), which leaves real room to cover ad costs. This is why higher-priced plans matter so much. None of HighLevel, Kajabi or ManyChat publishes churn figures. Refunds and chargebacks are also commonly deducted from current or future commissions ([Sendcloud terms example](https://www.sendcloud.com/en-uk/affiliate-program-terms/)).

**Terms change, so plan around that.** Since 2024, Kajabi, Teachable, Kartra, Simplero and GetResponse have moved away from lifetime or flat-rate commissions. Canva closed its affiliate program in January 2024 ([Affiliate Times](https://affiliate-times.com/convertkits-rebrand-to-kit-what-affiliates-need-to-know-in-2026/)). An ad strategy that earns its money back only after a year or more is exposed to a rule change before then.

## Conclusion

The real filter is not the percentage. It is the **commission rate times the plan price times how long the customer stays**, compared against roughly $800-$1,300 in ad spend per paying customer. On that measure, HighLevel's $297+ plans are the only option in this research that clearly covers paid-ads costs with a margin. ClickFunnels Scale and Mighty Networks Scale come next, while the $99 tier (Skool, Thinkific, LearnWorlds) is close to break-even unless your pages convert better than average. The industry trend is also running against lifetime commissions. Kajabi, Teachable, Kartra and GetResponse have all moved to tiers or caps, so the lifetime programs you pick today may change their terms later.

Before spending any money, take four steps. First, open each shortlisted program's official affiliate terms and confirm the rate, duration, cookie and paid-search rules; the conflicts flagged above are for Mighty's 30% vs 20%, Thinkific's cookie, ClickFunnels' tiers, Systeme.io's 60% and SE Ranking's prices. Second, email the affiliate manager to approve your exact Google Ads plan in writing. Third, look up real keyword prices in Google Keyword Planner. Fourth, start with a small test budget aimed at a real comparison page, and measure your own click-to-paying-customer rate before you scale.
