# Follow the legal peptides, not the vials

People make money selling peptides online mostly through affiliate links and discount codes. A creator posts on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, a podcast or a paid Skool or Telegram community and earns a cut of each sale. The products fall into three groups that look alike online but are treated very differently by the law. **"Research use only" (RUO) vendors** sell injectable vials such as BPC-157, TB-500 and "research" semaglutide or retatrutide. They pay the richest commissions, usually **10–30% of each sale**, but they are the riskiest by far: the FDA says the "not for human consumption" label does not protect them, several of the biggest vendors shut down or were raided in 2025–2026, and independent lab tests rate some of the most heavily promoted ones poorly. **Telehealth and compounded-prescription programs** (Hims & Hers, Ro, Henry Meds, Hone Health) pay a flat **$10–$75 per new customer** on mainstream affiliate networks. They are legal when run properly, but the FDA has been sending warning letters about the kind of marketing claims affiliates tend to write. **Cosmetic and supplement peptide brands** (collagen powders, peptide skincare) pay less, roughly **3–25%**, but carry almost no regulatory risk. My recommendation: build on the cosmetic and supplement brands first, add mainstream telehealth programs only if you strictly avoid medical claims, and stay out of RUO vendors altogether. Two caveats before you rely on any of this. First, almost every commission figure here came from search-engine snippets or affiliate aggregator sites read on October 6, 2026, not from the brands' own terms pages, so check each one in the network dashboard before you join. Second, this is not legal advice. Anyone serious about this niche should have an FDA/FTC regulatory lawyer review their plan and their affiliate contracts.

## Three kinds of "peptide" business carry very different risk

The word "peptide" covers products with completely different legal status, and getting this distinction right is most of the decision. The research notes sort the market into three categories, summarized below ([business models notes, citing FDA and law-firm sources](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy)).

| Category | What is sold | Legal status (US, Oct 2026) | Typical affiliate pay | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RUO "research" vendors** | Injectable vials: BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu, "research" semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide | Unapproved new drugs when meant for people; the label is no defense | 10–30% of sale, some "lifetime" | Very high: legal, payment, vendor collapse |
| **Telehealth / compounded prescriptions** | Compounded GLP-1s, sermorelin and similar, prescribed by a licensed clinician and filled by a pharmacy | Legal if structured correctly, but rules changed fast in 2025–2026 | $10–$75 flat per new subscriber (mainstream); some unknown GLP-1 offers list $187–$352 | Moderate: marketing-claim enforcement |
| **Cosmetic / supplement peptides** | Collagen peptide powders, copper-peptide and peptide skincare | Ordinary consumer goods | 3–25% of sale | Low |

The key legal idea is **"intended use."** The FDA decides whether something is a drug by looking at the whole website, not the label alone. In August 2026 it warned five online vendors (Peak Performance Peptides, Royal Peptides, NuScience Peptides, Peptide Partners and Tex Peptides) whose products were all labeled "for research, laboratory, and analytical use" and "not for veterinary or human use." The FDA still found the products were meant for people because the sites carried **dosing information, injection instructions and dose calculators** ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy); [McDermott Will & Schulte](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/)). This matters for affiliates because peptide creators publish exactly that kind of content: dosing "protocols," reconstitution how-tos and personal results.

The 2026 headlines about peptides being "legalized" are easy to misread. In April 2026 the FDA took 12 peptides, including BPC-157, off its "Category 2" list of substances that compounding pharmacies are discouraged from using ([Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-advisory-committee-set-weigh-taking-action-certain-unapproved-peptides); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-considers-adding-a-dozen-peptides-to-its-bulk-drug-compounding-list.html)). In July 2026 an FDA advisory panel voted to recommend six peptides, **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon and Semax**, for the list licensed pharmacies may compound from. The BPC-157 vote was a narrow 8–6, and it went against the FDA's own staff scientists ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisory-committee-votes-add-popular-peptide-bpc/story?id=134913891); [NCPA](https://ncpa.org/newsroom/qam/2026/07/31/fda-advisory-committee-nominates-six-peptides-pharmacies-compound)). One outlet's headline counts four peptides rather than six ([PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-votes-loosen-restrictions-four-peptides)). Either way, the vote is advisory only. As of early September 2026 **no proposed rule had been published**, and any change would apply only to licensed pharmacies filling prescriptions. It does not make retail "research" sales legal ([Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/fda-voted-yes-on-six-peptides-now-what-the-regulatory-road-ahead); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)). The notes found no confirmed FDA action between the July vote and October 6, 2026, so check for news before you rely on this.

## Creators earn through codes, bounties and paid communities

The research found about seven ways people make money from peptides online. In rough order of how much capital they need, from least to most:

**Affiliate and creator discount codes for RUO vendors.** This is the most visible model. Creators often say they are "not sponsored" while linking to a seller with a code that pays them a cut, and they use slang like "peppers" or "the pepper community" to get past content moderation ([Celine Gounder](https://www.celinegounder.com/p/peptides-tiktok-affiliates-misinformation); [TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/gray-market-peptides-flood-tiktok-as-pharmacists-warn-of-safety-risks)). Saying "not sponsored" while earning on a code runs straight into the FTC's disclosure rules, covered below.

**Telehealth affiliate bounties.** These pay a flat fee for each new subscriber. They run through mainstream networks, mainly **Impact**, plus ShareASale, FlexOffers and Katalys for Hims ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/hims/); [Katalys](https://katalys.com/brands/hims-affiliate-program/)). An affiliate network also lists much higher GLP-1 bounties from less familiar brands, such as TrimRX at $187.50 and Gala Tirzepatide at $352 per sale ([WOWtrk](https://www.wowtrk.com/offer/2035752)). The notes could not verify those individual offer pages.

**Owning the store.** Some people launch their own RUO brand using powder bought from Chinese manufacturers or finished vials bought from US wholesalers. One source claims a milligram of BPC-157 can cost under $1 to make and sell for $30–$80 ([RethinkPeptides](https://rethinkpeptides.com/articles/for-research-use-only-the-legal-fiction-of-gray-market-peptides)), but that comes from a single unverified source. Others build a **white-label telehealth brand** on infrastructure like OpenLoop, which supplies licensed clinicians, pharmacy fulfillment and payments and says no medical background is needed. Its pricing is not public ([OpenLoop](https://openloophealth.com/?p=573)).

**Clinics, med spas and coaches.** These run on the prescription side. One startup-cost template estimates about **$746K** for a physical clinic, but it comes from a vendor that sells financial-model templates, and some of its assumptions look inflated ([Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/startup-costs/peptide-therapy)).

**Paid communities and "group buys."** One Skool directory lists a peptide community with about 1,571 members at $10 a month and a group-buy community with 713 members at $25 a month, which works out to roughly $15K in monthly revenue each ([Skoolmakers](https://skoolmakers.com/communities/)). These numbers are anecdotal. Group buys, where members pool orders straight from Chinese manufacturers, are usually organized on Telegram ([Gray.guide](https://www.gray.guide/sourcing-details)).

**Ordinary collagen and skincare affiliate marketing.** This runs through Amazon, deal sites and direct-to-consumer brands ([Hip2Save](https://hip2save.com/deals/vital-proteins/)). It is a normal, lower-margin affiliate niche.

The RUO side runs on workarounds because every mainstream channel shuts it out. Google's and Meta's ad policies prohibit promoting unapproved drugs ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595718?hl=en); [Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/)). Amazon removed many BPC-157 and TB-500 listings in December 2025 ([BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/worlds-largest-retailers-selling-unapproved-peptides-and-performance-enhancing-drugs)). Stripe, PayPal and Shopify Payments reportedly shut peptide merchants down and hold their funds for up to 180 days, though that detail comes from high-risk payment processors that have a reason to stress the danger ([InclusivePay](https://inclusivepay.com/peptide-payment-processor-shutdown/)). As a result, payments have moved to crypto. Chainalysis counted about **$32M in crypto paid to peptide vendors in Q1 2026, roughly 700% more than a year earlier**, and projects more than $100M for the year ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/gray-market-peptide-crypto-boom/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/peptides-crypto-looksmaxxing-economy-chainalysis-report-100-million/)). That figure counts crypto only, so total sales are higher. The typical RUO path to a sale runs from a coded short video, to a link-in-bio or private community, to a vendor site with a code, to payment by crypto or a high-risk processor. The notes found **no verified, journalist-sourced income figures for individual peptide affiliates**, so treat any "I make $X a month" claim you see online with suspicion.

## RUO vendors pay 10–30% but keep collapsing under enforcement

On paper, the research-peptide programs look like the best deals. The table below shows the terms found, all from search snippets on October 6, 2026, with the vendors' own pages unopened. Payout thresholds, payment methods and approval rules were mostly not visible.

| Vendor | Reported terms | Platform | Independent test rating (Finnrick) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swiss Chems | 20% per sale ([Swiss Chems](https://swisschems.is/affiliate-program/)) | In-house | **48%, ranked 279th of 283** ([Finnrick](https://www.finnrick.com/vendors/swiss-chems)) |
| Limitless Life Nootropics | 15% most peptides, 12% GLP-1s ([Limitless](https://limitlesslifenootropics.com/affiliate-registration/)) | In-house | **56%** ([Finnrick](https://www.finnrick.com/vendors/limitless-life-nootropics)) |
| Apollo Peptide Sciences | Up to 30%, 90-day cookie ([Refersion](https://apollopeptides.refersion.com)) | Refersion | Not retrieved |
| Data Peptides | 20% lifetime per customer ([dub.co](https://partners.dub.co/data-peptides)) | dub.co | Not retrieved |
| CertaPeptides | 15–25% ([Certa](https://certapeptides.com/affiliate)) | In-house | Not retrieved |
| Real Peptides | 10%, 90-day cookie ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/125918)) | Awin (rare mainstream listing) | Not retrieved |
| Core Research Peptides | 10%, 30-day tracking ([Core](https://coreresearchpeptides.com/affiliates/)) | In-house | Not retrieved |

The bigger problem is that these businesses keep disappearing. Amino Asylum went offline after a reported federal raid around **June 18, 2025** and was still down in March 2026 ([Roidly](https://roidly.com/2026/03/15/amino-asylum-alternative/)). Search snippets said its affiliates were still owed commissions, though that is unverified. **Peptide Sciences**, reportedly one of the largest vendors, shut down on **March 6, 2026** with no explanation ([Project Biohacking](https://projectbiohacking.beehiiv.com/p/peptide-sciences-just-closed-its-doors)). Science.bio closed in January 2026, Strate Labs in July and Eternal Peptides in September, according to vendor-tracker sites that are themselves of low reliability ([ThePeptideCatalog](https://thepeptidecatalog.com/articles/peptide-vendors-shut-down-2025-2026); [ChemVerify](https://www.chemverify.com/learn/research-peptide-vendor-shutdown-wave-2026)). Some sources say Amino Asylum's founders pleaded guilty. A fact-check says the documented guilty pleas actually belong to a different company, **Paradigm Peptides**, and that no charges against Amino Asylum have been made public ([PeptideDigest](https://peptidedigest.co/amino-asylum-raid-what-happened)). An affiliate tied to a vendor that vanishes loses the income stream, and possibly the unpaid commissions too.

Enforcement has grown heavier. A Michigan man was sentenced to **21 months** in July 2026 for a conspiracy to bring unapproved drugs, including peptides, in from China ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmi/pr/2026_0723_Piper_Sentencing_PR)). A Utah physician was indicted in April 2026 on charges that include aiding and abetting the sale of misbranded peptides from China to more than 200 patients ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china)). In August 2026 Eli Lilly sued six sellers of "research" retatrutide and said it had reported **more than 14,000 websites, ads, posts and listings** to platforms ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/g-s1-138594/eli-lilly-files-6-lawsuits-against-businesses-selling-black-market-obesity-drug)). State pharmacy boards in Ohio and Alabama have also acted ([McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/federal-regulators-and-state-boards-crack-down-on-ruo-peptides/)). Retatrutide and "research" GLP-1s are the most dangerous corner, because they draw FDA letters and drug-maker lawsuits at the same time.

Product quality is a reputational risk as well as a legal one. Finnrick, an independent tester that says it sells no peptides and takes no payment to change ratings, requires at least 99.5% purity and a fill within ±35% of the label ([Finnrick](https://preview.finnrick.com/vendors/precision-peptide-co)). By that standard, Swiss Chems, one of the most heavily promoted affiliate vendors, passed **4 of 10** classifiable tests ([Finnrick](https://www.finnrick.com/vendors/swiss-chems)). Coverage of seized gray-market peptides reported that **30% had the wrong ingredient** and two-thirds failed basic purity standards ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/crypto-payments-to-gray-market-peptide-vendors-surge-700-in-2026)), although the original lab study was not identified. Be wary of glowing vendor reviews too. A "9.4/10, top 1%" rating for Core Peptides came from a site with apparent commercial ties, in a format Finnrick does not use ([PeptideStack](https://peptidestack.io/blog/core-peptides-review-2026)).

**Can the affiliate personally get in trouble?** In principle, yes. The notes found **no public case charging a pure affiliate or influencer** over peptide promotion. But several legal routes could reach one. Federal drug law covers anyone who "causes" misbranded drugs to be sold, and prosecutors have used aiding-and-abetting and conspiracy charges in peptide cases ([DOJ Utah](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china); [21 U.S.C. §333](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/333)). The FDA has sent a warning letter to an affiliate-network site for unapproved drugs before ([FDA: PharmacyAffiliates.org](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/pharmacyaffiliatesorg-08162018)). Health-law firms warn that sending consumers to unapproved peptide sites can be treated as aiding distribution ([DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/can-you-use-influencers-to-promote-peptides/)). FTC rules apply on top of all this. Affiliates must clearly disclose commissions, and health claims need solid scientific backing ([FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). The FTC's fake-review rule, in effect since October 2024, allows penalties of about **$53,000 per violation** for misrepresented testimonials, and influencers can be liable for misrepresenting their own experience with a product ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). In the Teami tea case, the FTC also sent warning letters to the paid influencers themselves ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/03/tea-marketer-misled-consumers-didnt-adequately-disclose-payments-well-known-influencers-ftc-alleges)). A personal "it fixed my knee" post is risky twice over: it is evidence that the product is meant for people, and it is a testimonial the FTC can scrutinize.

## Telehealth programs pay flat bounties, but ad copy draws FDA fire

The prescription route is the legitimate version of the peptide boom, and it is where the money may move if compounding rules loosen. Bloomberg describes a "multibillion-dollar" startup race ahead of expected legalization ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-peptide-legalization-gold-rush/)). Hims & Hers alone reported about **$2.35B in 2025 revenue** and roughly 2.5M subscribers ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hims-hers-bullish-weight-loss-business-despite-glp-1-market-shift)).

| Brand | Reported payout | Network | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hims / Hers | $20–$40 per new subscription, 30-day cookie, usually one-time ([commissiondex](https://commissiondex.com/programs/hims/)); Hers about $20 ([OffersPilot](https://www.offerspilot.com/hers-affiliate-program-review-earn-20-per-sale-promoting-a-trusted-womens-health-brand/)) | Impact, ShareASale, FlexOffers, Katalys | Received FDA warning letters, Sept 2025 |
| Ro | $10, 3–20% per sale, $5–$10 per lead ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/ro/)) | Impact | Aggregator data, undated |
| Henry Meds | $25 per subscription ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/henry-meds/)) | Impact | Aggregator data |
| Hone Health | $30–$75 per acquisition ([PrescribedRx](https://prescribedrx.com/hone-health-affiliate-program/)) | Impact only | Men's hormones |

All of these figures come from aggregators and may be out of date, because the official Impact terms are visible only after you log in. Watch for lookalike companies. Search results showing a 20% recurring rate for "Mochi" belong to a Philippine company, **not Mochi Health** ([mochi.ph](https://www.mochi.ph/legal-docs/mochi-affiliate-program-terms-and-conditions)), and the "Eden 20–35% recurring" terms belong to eden.so, **not the tryeden telehealth company** ([eden.so](https://eden.so/affiliates/)). A $300–$400 bounty attributed to tryeden had no source page and should be treated as unverified. Peptide-specific clinics such as Live Vital, PeRx, Strut Health and Ways2Well showed no public affiliate terms ([HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/peptide-telehealth/)).

The risk in this category is the **marketing claims**, not the product. In September 2025 the FDA cited Hims & Hers for calling compounded semaglutide the "same active ingredient as Ozempic and Wegovy" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hims-hers-health-inc-dba-hims-716567-09092025)). In early 2026 it sent a batch of 30 warning letters to telehealth firms over similar "sameness" claims. The notes date this batch differently, February 20 in one source and March 3 in another ([Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/fda-warns-telehealth-companies-over-marketing-of-compounded-glp-1-weight-loss-drugs/); [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters)). Also in February 2026, HHS referred Hims & Hers to the Department of Justice ([Drug Topics](https://www.drugtopics.com/view/fda-issues-warnings-to-30-telehealth-companies-against-illegal-marketing-of-compounded-glp-1-medications)). In May 2026 the FDA proposed blocking large-scale compounding of semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide, and whether that proposal has been finalized is unknown ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/01/2026-08552/list-of-bulk-drug-substances-for-which-there-is-a-clinical-need-under-section-503b-of-the-federal)). An affiliate who repeats "same as Ozempic" copy takes on the same claim risk the brand does. The GLP-1 product line these programs depend on could also shrink.

## Collagen and skincare brands are the low-risk lane

The cosmetic and supplement side pays less per sale and uses shorter cookies (the window after a click during which a purchase still earns you credit). In exchange, the programs sit on mainstream networks that accept new publishers, the brands are not shutting down overnight, and the notes found no enforcement issues.

| Brand | Reported terms | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Ancient Nutrition** | **15% / 20% / 25% tiers** by monthly sales, $150+ welcome kit, product stipends ([Ancient Nutrition](https://ancientnutrition.com/pages/affiliate)) | Direct (network listings show only 2.8–5%: [Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/54585)) |
| Vital Proteins | 8%, 7-day cookie (US) ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/vital-proteins-affiliate-program/)) | FlexOffers |
| The Ordinary / DECIEM | Up to 10%, 30-day cookie ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/the-ordinary-affiliate-program/)) | FlexOffers, Awin |
| Medik8 | 10%, 30-day cookie ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/medik8/)) | Awin (UK-focused; US availability unconfirmed) |
| Amazon Associates | 3% Beauty and Health & Personal Care, 10% Luxury Beauty ([Lasso](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/)) | Amazon |

Ancient Nutrition's direct program is the standout. Its page was the only first-party commission page in the notes, and its top tier rivals the RUO vendors' rates without the legal baggage. Signing up directly appears to pay several times more than its network listings, so compare both before you choose. The market is real if not spectacular. Copper-peptide skincare alone is estimated at about $285M in 2024 ([DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/copper-peptide-skincare-market-71167)). Collagen-peptide estimates vary too widely between research firms to rely on any one number ([Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/collagen-peptide-market-industry-analysis)). The rules here are ordinary affiliate hygiene: disclose every commission clearly and keep skincare claims cosmetic ("smoother-looking skin") rather than medical ("heals").

**Recommended order of preference.** Start with Ancient Nutrition (direct), The Ordinary/DECIEM, Vital Proteins and Amazon, plus Medik8 if your audience is in the UK. Second, and only with strict copy discipline (no dosing, no "same as Ozempic," no before-and-after promises), add mainstream telehealth programs on Impact: Hone Health, Henry Meds, Ro and Hims & Hers, keeping in mind that Hims is under active regulatory scrutiny. Skip the high-bounty GLP-1 offers from unfamiliar brands on niche networks. **Avoid RUO vendors entirely**, whatever the commission. That includes Swiss Chems, Limitless Life, Apollo, the various "Core" sites and every retatrutide or "research GLP-1" seller. The high rates do not make up for the combination of legal exposure, payment-platform bans, vendors that close without paying, and products that independent labs fail.

## Conclusion

The peptide affiliate economy rewards risk in an unusual way. The programs that pay most are the ones most likely to disappear, take your unpaid commissions with them and leave your content as evidence. The lasting money is moving toward the prescription channel, not away from it. If the FDA eventually lets pharmacies compound BPC-157 and its peers, demand that RUO vendors now serve will shift to licensed telehealth providers. Those providers run normal, network-tracked affiliate programs, and an affiliate with a clean, claim-free content library will be well placed to promote them. So the strategic choice is less "which vendor pays 20%" than "which audience and reputation will still be usable in 2027." That points to building an audience around legal collagen and skincare now, while watching the FDA rulemaking.

Several items need checking before any decision. Confirm each program's terms inside its network dashboard, since nearly all figures here are unverified snippets. Look for FDA action on the six peptides after the July 2026 panel vote, and on the proposed GLP-1 compounding ban. For tax, treat commissions as self-employment income and confirm current reporting thresholds with a CPA, because the notes could not verify the 2026 rules. Finally, have an FDA/FTC regulatory lawyer review your business model and any affiliate agreement before signing, because these contracts often contain indemnity clauses that shift the brand's liability onto the affiliate.
