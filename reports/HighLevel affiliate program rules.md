# Send HighLevel ads to your own page

**Short answer: no, you cannot point a Google ad straight at your HighLevel affiliate link.** HighLevel's affiliate terms say affiliates may not send people "to HighLevel's Website from any PPC ad or [use] redirects that yield the same result; customers must be directed to an actual page on the affiliate's Website" ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)) [official snippet]. A "PPC ad" is any pay-per-click ad, which includes Google Ads. So the ad has to land on a real page that you own, with real content and an affiliate disclosure, and your affiliate link goes on that page. The same terms ban the brand names HighLevel, GoHighLevel, GHL and SaaSPRENEUR as keywords and in ad text. They also ban competitors' brand names, offering your own trials or discounts without written approval, and outranking HighLevel's own ads in positions 1-5 ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. Breaking these rules can get you removed from the program and cost you every commission not yet paid. The money is worth protecting: HighLevel pays **40% of the monthly subscription for as long as the customer stays**, which is **$118.80 a month on the popular $297 plan**. **One important limit on this research:** the researchers could not open any gohighlevel.com page. Every fact here comes from search-engine excerpts, labelled **[official snippet]** when the excerpt came from a HighLevel page and **[third-party]** when it came from a blog or review site. Read the live agreement before you spend money on ads.

## Do / Don't compliance checklist

This is the one-page version. The sections below explain each point and give the sources.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Send every paid ad to **your own page on your own domain**, such as a review, tutorial, comparison or free-guide page | Point an ad, or a redirect, straight at your affiliate link or gohighlevel.com |
| Use **generic keywords** such as "CRM for marketing agencies", "white label CRM" or "all-in-one marketing software" | Bid on HighLevel, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, gohighlevel.com, misspellings, or "coupon / promo / discount / deal / sale" versions |
| Add the brand names (and misspellings) as **negative keywords** so broad and phrase match never trigger them | Put the brand names in the ad headline, ad text, display name or display URL |
| Put a **clear disclosure** near the top of your page saying you earn a commission | Bid on competitor brands, for example "ClickFunnels alternative" or "HubSpot vs" |
| Keep claims **truthful and based on your own experience** | Promise income or results, or call HighLevel a "business opportunity", franchise or "business-in-a-box" |
| Get **written permission** from HighLevel before any special offer, contest or giveaway | Invent your own trial, discount, coupon or bonus offer that names HighLevel |
| Set up **Tipalti** in your affiliate dashboard so payouts can reach you | Buy HighLevel through your own link (self-referral) |
| Follow anti-spam and telemarketing law (CAN-SPAM, TCPA) | Send unsolicited mass emails, texts or DMs |
| Watch your ad position and keep below HighLevel's own ads | Appear above HighLevel in ad positions 1-5 on any search term |
| | Use cookie stuffing, pop-ups, forced redirects or masked links, or let anyone else use your link |

## HighLevel sells rebrandable marketing software to agencies

HighLevel is an all-in-one sales and marketing system for small businesses. It replaces a stack of separate tools: a customer database (CRM), sales funnels and websites, email and text messaging, booking calendars, review requests, and AI chat and voice assistants. HighLevel describes itself as "the AI-powered operating system designed to help agencies and SMBs build, operate and scale" ([StreetInsider press release](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/How+HighLevel+Customers+Built+Real+Momentum+in+2025/26087899.html)) [official snippet]. One agency account manages many "sub-accounts", one for each client business ([HighLevel developer docs](https://marketplace.gohighlevel.com/docs/2023-02-21/oauth/AgencyVsSubAccount)) [official snippet]. Clients often see the neutral name **LeadConnector** on the app, phone and email services instead of HighLevel ([The Tech Tribe](https://help.thetechtribe.com/support/solutions/articles/48001226237-what-is-leadconnector)) [third-party].

**The core buyers are digital marketing agencies and consultants.** They "purchase HighLevel licenses, white-label the platform under their own branding and custom domains, and resell access and services" to small-business clients ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/How+HighLevel+Customers+Built+Real+Momentum+in+2025/26087899.html)) [official snippet]. Coaches, course creators, freelancers and single local businesses also buy it, usually on the cheapest plan ([Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing)) [third-party]. HighLevel reports a community of **"over 2 million businesses"**, a figure that counts agencies' client accounts, not just paying customers ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/How+HighLevel+Customers+Built+Real+Momentum+in+2025/26087899.html)) [official snippet]. Customers tend to stay because every client's contacts, automations, funnels and phone numbers live inside the platform, so leaving means rebuilding everything. That is the researchers' reasoning from how the product is built; no churn figures were found.

The usual complaints are a steep learning curve (reviewers estimate 6-8 weeks to feel confident), uneven support, and surprise usage charges for texts and AI ([autogencrm](https://autogencrm.com/?p=3950); [oneexpand review](https://oneexpand.com/gohighlevel-review/); [velpoint](https://velpoint.it.com/reviews/)) [third-party]. These are worth addressing honestly on your landing page, since the policies require truthful claims.

## Ads must land on your page, never on HighLevel

### What the terms say about direct linking and keywords

The direct-link ban is explicit. Affiliates may not link "to HighLevel's Website from any PPC ad", including through redirects, and "customers must be directed to an actual page on the affiliate's Website" ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)) [official snippet]. Some affiliate guides recommend sending traffic "directly to the GHL trial page" ([autogencrm](https://autogencrm.com/gohighlevel-affiliate-marketing/)) [third-party]. That works for a free blog post or video, but **for a paid ad it breaks the contract**.

The banned keyword list is longer than most people expect. Without HighLevel's written consent you may not bid on its **"Restricted Terms": HighLevel, HighLevel Inc, gohighlevel.com, www.gohighlevel.com, HighLevel coupon, HighLevel discount code, HighLevel discount, HighLevel promo code, HighLevel sale, HighLevel promo, HighLevel sales, HighLevel deals, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, "and any other terms that HighLevel may deem a Restricted Term in the future."** The ban covers "variations or misspellings", applies on "Google, Bing, MSN, Yahoo, Facebook or any other network", and also bars these words "in ad title, ad copy, display name or as the display URL" ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)) [official snippet]. You also cannot combine a restricted term with other words, so "GoHighLevel review" or "best GHL alternative" is out ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet].

Two further rules catch people out. First, affiliates may not "bid in any manner appearing higher than HighLevel for any search term in positions 1-5" in any pay-per-click auction. Second, they may not use any technique "that generates paid search results based on any trademarks of HighLevel ... or based on the trademarks or brand name of any competitor of HighLevel" ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. Read plainly, the second rule rules out keywords like "ClickFunnels alternative", "Kajabi vs" or "HubSpot alternative". The first means that on any generic term where HighLevel also advertises, your ad must sit below HighLevel's.

Offers are restricted too. Affiliates may not offer "discounts, coupons, promo codes, free trials, prizes, or other incentives related to HighLevel unless expressly authorized in writing," and may not run contests or giveaways that mention HighLevel without approval ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. Many affiliate sites say HighLevel affiliate links unlock a **30-day trial instead of the standard 14 days** ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-30-day-free-trial/); [automatethejourney](https://automatethejourney.com/blog/gohighlevel-free-trial-2026)) [third-party]. No HighLevel page confirmed this, and it is unclear whether writing "30-day free trial" in an ad counts as an offer that needs written approval.

### Google's own rules point the same way

Google rejects ads whose landing pages are "solely designed to send users to another site (including bridge pages, doorways, gateways, or other intermediate pages)" or whose content is copied "without adding value" ([Google: Insufficient original content](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427718?hl=en)) [official snippet]. Landing pages "must offer unique value to users" ([Google: Destination requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6368661?hl=en)), and an ad is rejected when the domain shown in the ad does not match where the click actually lands ([Google: Destination mismatch](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16428020?hl=en)) [official snippet]. Affiliates also may not use a product's brand name as their advertiser business name ([Google: Business name requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14847993?hl=en)) [official snippet]. Google sometimes lets resellers mention a brand in ad text ([Google: Trademarks](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118?hl=en)), but HighLevel's contract is stricter, and the stricter rule is the one that protects your commissions.

## How to run Google Ads for HighLevel safely

The compliant setup has four parts. Each follows from the rules above. The keyword and page ideas are the researchers' reading of those rules, not HighLevel guidance.

**1. Build a real page on your own domain.** Write something useful: an honest review of what an agency can do with an all-in-one CRM, a setup tutorial, a comparison of approaches (not of named competitor brands in your ads), a case study from your own use, or a free checklist or template in exchange for an email address. Put a **clear commission disclosure near the top**. The Policies require you to disclose "clearly and conspicuously, meaning your audience must be able to easily see and understand that you will earn a commission if they make a purchase through your link" ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. A simple line works: "I earn a commission if you sign up through my link, at no extra cost to you." On Facebook and Instagram the disclosure must sit at the start of the caption, before the "read more" cut-off ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. Your affiliate link from the dashboard then goes in the page's buttons. It can name HighLevel; the brand-name ban covers ads and keywords, not your own page content, but keep every claim truthful.

**2. Choose generic keywords only.** Examples that avoid every restricted and competitor name include "CRM for marketing agencies", "white label CRM", "agency SaaS platform", "all-in-one marketing software", "SMS marketing for agencies" and "funnel builder". Add HighLevel, GoHighLevel, GHL, SaaSPRENEUR, "go high level", "gohighlevel" and common misspellings as **negative keywords** at account level. HighLevel reportedly recommends exactly this ([NetPartners](https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-affiliate-brand-bidding-ppc-policy/)) [third-party], and it stops broad match from showing your ad on brand searches by accident. Also exclude competitor brand names.

**3. Write ad text that describes the category, not the brand.** For example: "White-label CRM for agencies. Funnels, SMS, booking and reviews in one tool. Read the full walkthrough." Do not use HighLevel or GHL anywhere in headlines, descriptions, the display path or your advertiser name. Leave out trial lengths, discounts and bonuses unless HighLevel has approved them in writing.

**4. Keep below HighLevel in the auction.** On any term where HighLevel's own ad appears, avoid outranking it in positions 1-5. In practice that means modest bids and checking the Auction Insights report in Google Ads, which shows when you appear above other advertisers. Researchers found no reliable cost-per-click figures for these specific keywords, so use Google Keyword Planner before setting a budget.

## Earning and keeping commissions: the full rulebook

### Commission per plan

The standard rate is **"40% monthly recurring commission from everyone you introduce to HighLevel"** ([HighLevel help: First Steps](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001182420-first-steps-to-be-a-successful-highlevel-affiliate)) [official snippet], paid "as long as referred accounts remain active". A second tier pays **5%** on customers brought in by affiliates you recruit; the current agreement reportedly applies it only "when HighLevel approves the second-tier designation" ([ghlfocus](https://ghlfocus.com/gohighlevel-affiliate-program/)) [third-party]. HighLevel's help centre gives the example of a $297 customer paying **$118.80 "every month they remain a customer"** ([HighLevel Support](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48000980325-become-a-highlevel-affiliate)) [official snippet, from earlier research]. Plan prices below are from third-party sites ([Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing); [rsla.io](https://rsla.io/blog/go-high-level-pricing)) [third-party]; the commission columns are simple arithmetic.

| Plan | Monthly price [third-party] | Your 40% (Tier 1) | 5% (Tier 2, if approved) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $97 | $38.80 / month | $4.85 / month |
| Unlimited | $297 | $118.80 / month | $14.85 / month |
| Agency Pro (formerly SaaS Pro) | $497 | $198.80 / month | $24.85 / month |

Annual plans cost about 17% less, roughly $80, $247 and $414 a month ([rsla.io](https://rsla.io/blog/go-high-level-pricing)) [third-party], so commission on annual customers is about 17% lower per month. The Tier 2 column assumes 5% of the customer's subscription. One snippet instead describes 5% of the recruited affiliate's *commissions*, which would be only 2% of the subscription. Nobody could confirm which is right.

### When a sale counts

A purchase is a **"Qualified Purchase" only if a customer "(1) clicks your unique Affiliate Link; (2) completes a new purchase; and (3) maintains their account in good standing for at least 45 days"** ([Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)) [official snippet]. An account paused during those 45 days does not qualify ([ghlbuilds](https://ghlbuilds.com/highlevel-affiliate-program-agreement/)) [third-party]. HighLevel "decides whether a purchase qualifies for a commission in its sole discretion" and can pause or withhold payments if it has concerns about a transaction ([Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)) [official snippet]. Credit reportedly goes to the **last affiliate link clicked within 90 days** ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/highlevel-affiliate-payouts-explained/)) [third-party], but older reviews say 30-60 days ([affililist](https://www.affililist.com/affiliate/gohighlevel)) [third-party], so treat cookie length as unconfirmed. If a paid commission is later refunded or charged back, it is deducted from future payouts ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/highlevel-affiliate-payouts-explained/)) [third-party].

### How and when you are paid

HighLevel "approves and sends all payments on the 15th of each month for all commissions earned the previous month", with a **$50 minimum**, and "all Payouts are through Tipalti. You must set up Tipalti in order to choose your payment method" ([HighLevel help: Affiliate Payouts](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001208136-affiliate-payouts-where-how-when-can-i-get-paid-)) [official snippet]. Tipalti offers direct deposit, PayPal or debit card ([botpenguin](https://botpenguin.com/blogs/gohighlevel-affiliate-program)) [third-party]. Two forfeiture rules matter. Older terms say that if combined commissions in a 120-day period do not exceed $50, they "will not be paid and will be forfeited" ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)) [official snippet]; whether that survives in the current agreement is unclear. Commissions may also be forfeited "if HighLevel is unable to submit payment to your payment account for any reason" ([Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)) [official snippet], so finish Tipalti setup early.

### Prohibited activities and conduct

Beyond the advertising rules above, the Policies and Agreement ban the following ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules); [Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)) [official snippet]:

- **Self-referral.** Buying HighLevel through your own link is "considered fraudulent activity that may result in termination and forfeiture of commissions."
- **Tracking fraud.** This covers cookie stuffing, pop-ups that set cookies, forced redirects, masked referral links, and claiming credit for sales you did not influence.
- **Spam.** No "unsolicited mass emails, texts, or social media messages". Comply with CAN-SPAM, the TCPA, state telemarketing laws and the FTC Endorsement Guides.
- **Dishonest claims.** "Never make false, misleading, or exaggerated claims about HighLevel, its products, or what users can achieve." Do not make claims "beyond the scope of marketing materials produced by HighLevel", and never call it "a business opportunity, franchise opportunity, a 'business-in-a-box,' or an assisted marketing plan."
- **Brand misuse.** Do not alter logos, use the name in a domain or social handle, or imply you are employed by HighLevel.
- **Sharing your link.** No sub-affiliate networks, and no letting a third party use your link, unless agreed in writing.
- **Disparagement.** No defamatory or disparaging statements about HighLevel or its staff.
- **Bad conduct.** Behaviour that "reflects poorly on the Program or the HighLevel community" can lead to removal.

### Termination and disputes

Breaking the Policies lets HighLevel "suspend Commission payments, revoke Program benefits, restrict event participation, or remove you from the Program and forfeit unpaid Commissions" ([Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules)) [official snippet]. If you are found out of compliance, termination can be immediate, "including forfeiture of earned commissions" ([HighLevel Affiliate Agreement](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms)) [official snippet]. Without any breach, either side can end the arrangement on 30 days' written notice ([Affiliate Terms (old)](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-terms-old)) [official snippet, legacy version]. Disputes go to "binding arbitration in Dallas, Texas" with a class-action waiver ([Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy)) [official snippet].

## What could not be confirmed, and what to ask HighLevel

**No gohighlevel.com page was read in full.** Every HighLevel site (the main site, help centre and affiliate portal) was blocked from the researchers' network, and so were web archives. "[Official snippet]" means a search engine's excerpt of the official page, which may be paraphrased or attached to the wrong HighLevel URL. Several pieces were not found at all: no official list of which products earn commission, no affiliate-specific inactivity rule, no country restrictions, no rule on how HighLevel announces changes, and no confirmation of the exact effective dates.

Before spending on ads, read the live [Affiliate Program Agreement](https://gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy) and [Program Policies](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliate-policy/program-rules) in full after logging into your dashboard. Then email HighLevel's affiliate support and **get written answers** to these questions:

1. **Trial offers in ads.** Can I mention the 30-day free trial (if my link gives one) in ad text and on my landing page, or does that need written authorization as a "free trial" incentive?
2. **Usage charges.** Do I earn commission on wallet and usage charges (texts, phone, email, AI Employee) and add-ons, or only on the base subscription?
3. **Tier 2 math.** Is the 5% second tier 5% of the recruited affiliate's customer's subscription, or 5% of that affiliate's commission? How do I get "second-tier designation" approved?
4. **Cookie length and attribution.** Is the window 90 days, last-click, or something else?
5. **Landing pages hosted in HighLevel.** Does a funnel or website I build inside my own HighLevel account, on my own domain, count as "an actual page on the affiliate's Website"?
6. **Current wording.** Is the PPC direct-linking ban from `/affiliate-terms` still in force under the current `/affiliate-policy` agreement, and does the 120-day / $50 forfeiture rule still apply?
7. **Positions 1-5.** How is this monitored on generic terms, and is a negative-keyword list for brand terms required or only recommended?

Keep the replies. HighLevel decides qualification "in its sole discretion", so a written answer from its own team is your best protection if an ad campaign is ever questioned.

## Conclusion

The direct-linking question has a clear answer, but it hides a broader point: **HighLevel's paid-ads rules are built so that affiliates add something HighLevel's own ads don't.** The banned keywords, the position 1-5 ceiling and the own-page requirement all push affiliates away from people already searching for HighLevel and toward people still deciding what kind of tool they need. That suits an affiliate with real content. An honest review or tutorial that ranks on "white label CRM" reaches buyers HighLevel's brand ads miss, and it also satisfies Google's original-content rules.

The biggest risk to your income is not a rejected ad. It is losing unpaid commissions after the fact, because HighLevel can decide at its sole discretion that a sale was non-compliant. With $118.80 a month at stake on each Unlimited customer, an hour spent reading the live agreement and getting written answers to the seven questions above costs less than a single lost referral.
