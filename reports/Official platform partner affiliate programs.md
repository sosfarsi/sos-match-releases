# HighLevel beats the badge-holders on payout

**Bottom line:** As of October 2026, the program that best matches every part of your brief is still **HighLevel (gohighlevel.com): 40% every month for as long as the customer stays, on plans of $97-$497 a month**, from a fast-growing company that **Meta picked as an early booking partner for Facebook Lead Ads**. **ManyChat's basic access to Facebook and Instagram is not unique.** It uses the same official Meta messaging tools that any approved software company can use, and competitors such as Chatfuel, respond.io, WATI and many others use them too. **There is one real exception: ManyChat has a Meta-granted head start on automatically DMing new Instagram followers ("Follow to DM").** Meta made ManyChat its exclusive *launch* partner for this gated beta in late 2025. ManyChat's own word for its position is "first", not "only", and Chatfuel now runs a limited alpha of the same feature. ManyChat's other advantages are its size, its brand and fresh funding: **$140M raised in April 2025, profitable, 1M+ businesses**. Its affiliate program, though, **pays for 12 months only**. **Skool** pays 40% for life on a $99 plan and has strong backing, including Alex Hormozi as a co-owner, but it publishes no user or revenue figures, so "how widely used" can only be estimated. The most surprising finding is a mismatch. **Tools with a confirmed official badge from TikTok, Google or Shopify (Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Later, Madgicx, Omnisend) mostly pay 15-20% for a limited time. Tools paying 25%+ for life (Localo, AdCreative.ai, Metricool, Publer) have no badge that could be confirmed.** Because the platform access is the same for everyone, choose on **company strength, plan price and commission length**, not on badges. **One important limit:** the researchers' network blocked them from opening any official page directly. Every figure comes either from search-engine excerpts of the company's own pages (labelled **Official (snippet)**) or from third-party directories and blogs (labelled **Third-party**). Confirm each number on the official affiliate page before you rely on it.

## ManyChat uses the same door as everyone, plus one Meta-granted head start

You asked whether ManyChat has special access to Facebook and Instagram. **For everyday messaging, it does not. For one feature, auto-DMing new Instagram followers, it currently does** (explained below). ManyChat describes itself as a Meta Business Partner "officially approved to work directly with Instagram and Facebook Messenger APIs," and says Meta reviews a partner's platform and security before granting that status and can take it away ([ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/what-it-means-to-be-a-facebook-business-partner/), Official (snippet)). An API is simply the official "plug" that lets outside software send and read messages on Facebook or Instagram. Meta's own developer documents show that **any software company can get the same messaging permission** after passing Meta's App Review ([Meta for Developers: App Review for Instagram API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review), Official (snippet)). Coverage of ManyChat's own launch noted that Instagram messaging support "is available for all developers" ([jotup.co, Third-party](https://jotup.co/node/1363410)).

Competitors use exactly the same door. Chatfuel calls itself an official Meta Business Partner covering Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and TikTok ([Chatfuel docs](https://chatfuel.com/docs/getting-started/supported-channels), Official (snippet)). For WhatsApp, Meta approves "Business Solution Providers" that resell access. Third-party lists name Twilio, 360dialog, WATI, AiSensy, Interakt, Gupshup, Infobip, Zoko, respond.io and Bird ([lilachbullock.com, Third-party](https://www.lilachbullock.com/whatsapp-business-api-providers-small-business/)). Even small tools such as CreatorFlow advertise themselves as "100% Meta-compliant" ([CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/es/affiliate-program), Official (snippet)). Being a ManyChat customer does not get around Meta's rules either. ManyChat's own community forum has users reporting that they were "ineligible for using Instagram business messaging API" ([ManyChat Community](https://community.manychat.com/general-q-a-43/ineligible-for-using-instagram-business-messaging-api-4581)).

There are really three different levels of "official." A **Meta Business Partner badge** is a trust and directory listing. A **Tech Provider** is a registered software company allowed to serve many businesses. A **WhatsApp Solution Provider** can actually sell WhatsApp access and handle its billing ([360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/get-started/tech-providers/understanding-the-meta-tech-provider-program), Official (snippet)). The researchers' reading is that ManyChat holds the first and works as the second for WhatsApp, but is **not commonly listed as a WhatsApp Solution Provider**. This is their inference, not a confirmed fact. **No one could check Meta's own partner directory**, so badge claims for every company except ManyChat and Chatfuel (both self-stated) are unconfirmed.

### The exception: "Follow to DM" for new Instagram followers

You pointed out that ManyChat can automatically message people who newly follow an Instagram account, and that other platforms cannot. **A fact check found this is partly true.** ManyChat offers "Follow to DM" (also called "Say Hi to New Followers"). ManyChat calls it a "Meta-approved feature" and says it is "the exclusive partner to bring it to market first" ([ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/introducing-follow-to-dm/); [ManyChat Community](https://community.manychat.com/product-updates/turn-every-follow-into-a-conversation-introducing-follow-to-dm-7628), Official (snippet)). Third parties date the launch to Meta's Instagram Summit on **October 22, 2025**. ManyChat's own pages did not confirm that date (Third-party). This really is something most competitors cannot do through official channels. **Meta's public Instagram tools include no "new follower" event.** The documented event types cover comments, mentions, messages and similar activity, but not follows ([Meta for Developers: Instagram webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/webhooks), Official (snippet); [Make.com community](https://community.make.com/t/instagram-business-module-webhooks-new-follower-event-trigger/16511), Third-party). The feature therefore runs on a **private, invitation-only Meta beta**, and ManyChat was the launch partner.

The claim that "no other platform can" goes too far, for five reasons:

1. **ManyChat's own word is "first", not "only".** That describes a launch head start, not permanent exclusivity.
2. **Chatfuel says its own version is "currently in Alpha and available to a limited group of creators"** ([Chatfuel docs](https://chatfuel.com/docs/instagram/dm-to-follow), Official (snippet)). Inro says Meta introduced follow-based messaging "as a limited beta" and that Inro has "built the groundwork" but is holding it back because the beta is unreliable ([Inro Help](https://help.inro.social/en/articles/15631961-can-i-auto-dm-new-followers-follow-to-dm), Official (snippet)).
3. **Meta, not ManyChat, decides which accounts qualify.** ManyChat's help article says "Eligibility for this automation is determined by Meta, not Manychat." It also says Meta allows only one such message per person per week across all accounts using the feature ([ManyChat Help](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/23096654243740-Follow-to-DM-on-Instagram-Say-Hi-to-New-Followers-BETA), Official (snippet)). Many ManyChat users report the trigger not firing ([ManyChat Community](https://community.manychat.com/general-q-a-43/1-000-usd-bounty-fix-manychat-new-follower-automation-issue-8331)).
4. **It is Instagram-only.** No evidence was found of a follow-triggered DM for Facebook Pages, on ManyChat or anywhere else.
5. **Unofficial tools such as Gramto advertise new-follower DMs too**, but they appear to work by logging into the account rather than through Meta's official tools ([Gramto help](https://gramto.ladesk.com/055632-How-to-Setup-Auto-DM-to-New-Followers), Official (snippet)). That is the researcher's inference. Instagram's terms ban automated access without permission, so these tools risk account penalties.

HighLevel has no such trigger; it is an open feature request ([HighLevel Ideas](https://ideas.gohighlevel.com/ad-reporting-and-attribution/p/trigger-of-new-instagram-follower), Official (snippet)). The fact-checker found no Meta-authored page that describes the feature or names ManyChat as exclusive. Overall confidence in this verdict is medium-high, and medium on the details, because no vendor or Meta page could be opened in full.

**What this means for you:** "Follow to DM" is a genuine selling point to mention when you promote ManyChat to Instagram creators, and it is **currently the main official way to do this**. Present it accurately, though: Instagram-only, Meta-approved account by account, and likely to reach competitors as Meta widens the beta. It makes ManyChat a better *product* for some buyers. It does not change its 12-month commission cap.

What ManyChat does have is scale and money. It claims to be "the world's largest Instagram DM Automation platform," with 100,000+ Instagram accounts ([ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/is-manychat-officially-approved-by-instagram/), Official (snippet)). It raised **$140M in April 2025, led by Summit Partners, and was already profitable** ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-22/social-media-marketing-startup-manychat-raises-140-million); [Summit Partners](https://www.summitpartners.com/news/manychat-raises-140m-to-fuel-the-future-of-ai-driven-customer-engagement-on-social-and-messaging-platforms)). It says it serves "more than a million businesses in 170+ countries," while TechCrunch cites about 1.5 million, both self-reported ([ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/manychat-raises-140m/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2996937)). Brands such as Nike and the New York Times use it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2996937)). For an affiliate, this means ManyChat is a safe, well-known product to recommend. Apart from "Follow to DM", a competitor with a better commission offers your customers the same Facebook and Instagram messaging features.

**Metricool needs a warning.** Its page promises commission "as long as a referral holds their Metricool Premium subscription, in year one and every year after," and says the click "never expires." If "up to $X per user" is a **lifetime total per customer**, Metricool is not really a lifetime program: you would stop earning after $100 per customer (or $300 at the higher tier). The pattern suggests a reason. Large, badge-holding companies can sell through agencies and their own sales teams, so they treat affiliates as a minor channel with small, time-limited payouts. Smaller or affiliate-led companies (HighLevel, Skool, Localo, AdCreative.ai) pay more and pay for longer because affiliates are a main way they grow. This is the researchers' interpretation, not a sourced fact.

**Thinkific** is the clearest example of a "watch closely" company. It is public on the Toronto Stock Exchange, so its numbers are audited. In 2025 it grew revenue 9% to **US$73.2M** and turned a small **$1.3M profit** ([Thinkific investor release](https://investors.thinkific.com/2026-03-05-Thinkific-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results)). This is not a failing company. It is profitable and cutting costs. But companies under cost pressure often trim their affiliate programs next, and Thinkific's 30% lifetime rate is generous for its size. If you promote Thinkific, assume its terms could change.

**Vendasta** cut 20 jobs (about 3%) in March 2026, citing AI, after earlier layoffs in 2022 ([CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/saskatoon/article/vendasta-cuts-20-jobs-in-saskatoon-as-company-pivots-to-ai/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/saskatoon/tech-company-vendasta-fires-5-per-cent-of-employees-1.6561893)). **Keap** was bought by Thryv for **$80M against about $85M in yearly revenue**, a low price that suggests it had stopped growing ([Thryv](https://www.thryv.com/news/thryv-holdings-announces-strategic-acquisition-of-keap/)).

**Kajabi** is a strong company: it was valued at **$2B+ in 2021** after a $550M raise ([OC Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/news/2021/may/10/kajabi-nabs-550m-knowledge-economy/)), and says its creators have earned **$10B+** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250806424975/en/$10B-in-Creator-Revenue-and-Climbing-Kajabi-Creators-Are-Achieving-Long-Term-Financial-Success)). Kajabi's own help centre (Official (snippet)) says new partners since **May 2025** get **100% of the first month for their first four referrals only, then 10% recurring at 5-24 active referrals, 15% at 25-49 and 20% at 50+** ([Kajabi Help](https://help.kajabi.com/articles/resources/kajabi-partner-program/partner-program-overview)). No search turned up lawsuits, major outages or announced affiliate cuts for any of these companies. Dedicated searches were not run, so that absence does not prove there are none.

## Conclusion

The question "who is officially partnered with Meta, Google, TikTok or Shopify?" turns out to matter less than it seems. The everyday official connections are open to any approved software company, so a badge mostly tells you a company passed a review. The exception is a gated Meta beta such as ManyChat's Instagram "Follow to DM": early access to a new feature can be a real but temporary edge. The companies that hold badges also tend to pay affiliates the least. What actually decides your income is a lifetime rate multiplied by a high plan price at a company likely to still be around, and still honouring the same terms, in two years. On that test, HighLevel is the standout, Skool and ClickFunnels follow, and Localo is a small but interesting option. ManyChat is a great company with an affiliate program built for one-year income, not lasting income.

The bigger risk is that terms change. Kajabi, Kartra and others have already cut lifetime payouts, and Thinkific's September 2026 layoffs show how cost pressure arrives. Before promoting anything, open each official affiliate page and settle the conflicts flagged above: ManyChat's $15 vs $39 price and 90 vs 120-day cookie; Chatfuel's 20% lifetime vs 30-40% for 12 months; Tidio's 30% vs 25% lifetime vs 20-25% for two years; SendPulse's 25% lifetime vs 30-40% for two years; Vendasta's 40% vs 20-30% for two years; Metricool's possible per-customer cap; Mighty's 30% vs 20%; and Systeme.io's 40% vs 60%. Ask each affiliate manager to confirm the duration in writing. A "lifetime" promise you cannot point to in the terms is not a lifetime promise.
